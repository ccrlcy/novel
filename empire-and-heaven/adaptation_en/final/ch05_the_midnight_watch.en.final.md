# Empire and Heaven (I)

## Chapter 5. The Midnight Watch

The cry of an owl came riding in on the wind through the olive grove. In the middle of the small room stood a square table, and on the table was a single lamp. The small flame fluttered from time to time and caught the shadows of the five people seated around the table, shaking them upon the wall.

The white-haired old man seated farthest inside, as seen from the door, looked at the man on his right with flashing eyes and asked in a low voice.

"And so... what was Bishop Clement's opinion?"

He did not look as old as the old man seated in the middle, but his hair and beard too were already quite gray.

"Bishop Clement of the church in Rome sent a letter to our church in Corinth not long ago. He was greatly worried about the fervent faith of the Corinthian church."

Then the younger man among the two seated toward the door looked at him and said,

"Bishop Sosthenes... I heard that fasting and abstaining from marriage are spreading in the Corinthian church, and that those who yearn for martyrdom are increasing. In a time when persecution is growing so severe and the Lord's return is being awaited so earnestly, would not such active faith be all the more necessary?"

The bishop of the Corinthian church, called Sosthenes, glanced once at the expression of the old man seated in the middle, and then opened his mouth.

"Of course Bishop Papias's words are right. The Lord also promised that he would send us the Holy Spirit, the Paraclete, and it cannot be denied that the many miracles and gifts that appeared when Paul and the apostles preached were all the coming of the Holy Spirit. But the matter of our Corinthian church is different...."

He paused for a moment, as if sinking into thought, and then looked at the mature man seated beside Bishop Papias.

"This Ephesus is also a trading port, but Corinth is an even more unusual place. Corinth is not only a place where many people come and go; it is also a place where corruption and decadence hold sway like rootless trees."

Corinth, originally a Greek trading port, was a crossroads connecting the north-south land route and the east-west sea route, so most of its citizens were people who had streamed in from elsewhere. The culture formed among such wayfarers was precisely Corinthian culture, the symbol of decadence and pleasure. If the grand Doric stone column was the vigor of morning, the simple and elegant Ionic stone column was the spirit of midday, and the Corinthian stone column, splendid but leaning toward technique, showed precisely the declining image of twilight.

"When Paul came to Corinth during his second missionary journey... he judged that Corinth was the very place that needed the gospel of Christ, and devoted his whole heart to evangelism. He happened to stay for a year and a half with the couple Aquila, who had moved from Rome, and labored to establish the church. Because it was a corrupt place, many people repented, and the Corinthian church at last rose up as if catching fire...."

"Bishop Sosthenes, you were the ruler of the Jewish synagogue at that time, were you not?"

"That is so. While listening to Paul's preaching, I came to understand the way of Christ and was baptized. The Jews of the synagogue in Corinth were alarmed by the church's growing power, and when they saw that even I had been baptized, they finally started a riot and accused us, and I was even beaten. But the problem came after Paul left."

After Paul left Corinth, the person who next took charge of evangelism in the Corinthian church was Apollos, who had moved from Ephesus. Apollos, who had always been eloquent, grew even deeper in faith after receiving instruction from Aquila and his wife in Ephesus, and captivated the people of the Corinthian church with moving sermons.

"After Paul left, strangely, factions began to arise in the Corinthian church. There was a Paul party, an Apollos party, a Peter party, and even something called a Christ party. It was partly because the Corinthians were drifters gathered from many places, but when I examined it carefully, the fundamental cause lay in the posture of the believers who were excessively fervent. That is, because they were too fervent, even small differences of opinion caused friction, and differences in habits of life or ways of thinking led immediately to conflict. In the end, that became the cause of factions and heretical belief."

The man seated beside Papias added a word.

"... It was then that Paul, who was in Ephesus, wrote a letter to the Corinthian church, was it not?"

"That is so, Bishop Onesimus. In that letter there are words advising them to seek the gifts, but to seek them according to love, and to put edification first even in prophecy and tongues."

Papias, the young man, also nodded.

"I remember reading a copy of that letter. He wrote that God is not a God of disorder, but of peace."

Then the old man of seventy, who had been seated opposite Bishop Sosthenes and only listening, opened his mouth.

"Paul's words seem to strike the heart of the matter. The gifts of the Holy Spirit should certainly be desired, but we must take note that we are now in a crisis even more serious than that...."

The white-haired old man who had been listening to their conversation nodded and looked at the seventy-year-old man who had just spoken.

"Bishop Ignatius... let us hear your opinion."

Then the old man called Ignatius assumed a respectful posture toward the old man seated inside, and opened his mouth carefully.

"As you know well, Apostle, since you yourself stayed in our Antioch church for as long as eleven years, there were many prophets there. The famous prophet Agabus, who prophesied the famine in Jerusalem during the time of Herod Agrippa and later even Paul's arrest, was also a man of Antioch; and are not all four daughters of Philip the deacon, who prophesy, from Antioch as well, though they now live in Hierapolis where Bishop Papias is? The early Antioch church was so full of the Holy Spirit, but there was no division among the believers. Later, when Paul and Barnabas set out on their missionary journey, there was a quarrel over whether Mark would accompany them, but it was not a serious division. Recently, however, problems have begun to arise even in the Antioch church. People keep beginning to make their own interpretations of Christ. This may perhaps be..."

Ignatius, bishop of the Antioch church, closed his mouth for a moment and gazed silently at the white-haired Apostle John.

The disheveled silver-white hair and the wrinkles deeply carved over his whole face had softened John's appearance considerably, but the intense light of his eyes, which flashed from time to time and shot at the other person, still reminded one that his old nickname had been "Son of Thunder."

Among the five people in the room, only Ignatius remembered John's youth. That burning ambition and fearfully hasty temperament now seemed to have ripened simply into a generous old man.

"This may perhaps be because the Lord's return is being delayed."

Sosthenes and Papias were nodding their agreement with those words.

"When the Lord received the disciples' questions about the end on the Mount of Olives in Jerusalem, after explaining in detail the things to come, he said that before this generation passed away, all these things would be accomplished. But already sixty-five years have passed since the Lord's resurrection, and all the apostles besides the Apostle John have passed away, and yet the Lord has still not come."

John was now listening to Ignatius's calm explanation with his eyes half closed.

"With the fall of Jerusalem under General Titus, part of the prophecy concerning the final tribulation was, in a sense, fulfilled. Yet even after that, the tribulations of the saints have not ceased. The great fire of Rome, the burial of Pompeii, events that seem to foreshadow the last days, continue without end, but we cannot know when the Lord will come. Moreover, has not the full-scale persecution of Emperor Domitian now begun again? Therefore, in order to explain why the Lord's return is delayed, perhaps new interpretations of the gospel have become necessary."

When Ignatius had explained this far, young Papias boldly interrupted again.

"As Bishop Ignatius says, it seems that the greatest crisis has come upon us Christians. Even if we have endured persecution from outside with love and passion for Christ, the more dreadful enemy seems to exist within us. What is more dreadful than swords and more dreadful than wild beasts is precisely the alteration of Christ. It seems that now we must stake our lives on guarding Christ."

John remembered Jesus' last request, which he had received on the shore of Tiberias.

-Guard my sheep.

Now, to John, who had lived a long life like a flame and was fading away, Papias was placing yet another heavy burden. It was precisely the demand that he defend Christ to the death.

Ignatius again continued speaking in a low voice.

"Deacon Nicholas, who was from the Antioch region, probably also began at first to reinterpret Christ out of some necessity. The problem of Deacon Nicholas seems likewise to have begun with excessive enthusiasm. Dualistic claims that the flesh is filthy and only the spirit is good, or a progressive attitude that there is no need for the law, ultimately became a trap that drove faith into disorder."

Onesimus, bishop of Ephesus, who until now had only listened to the bishops' conversation, carefully opened his mouth.

"The problem is that such thoughts are still branching out within the church."

Sosthenes, seated beside him, received his words as if he understood.

"That very man named Marcion came to see the Apostle, did he not?"

"That is so. Not long ago he came to the Apostle John and explained his ideas. It seems that his zeal for Christ and his respect for Paul were likewise excessive and led him into extreme thoughts. He denied the whole of the Old Testament and unfolded a thorough asceticism centered on celibate life, but when the Apostle judged that his thoughts were wrong, he flared up and left."

"They say that man is still going about here and there, gathering those who agree with him and establishing a new church."

"As he left after saying unpleasant things to the Apostle, he was cursed fiercely by the young brother Polycarp."

"Cursed fiercely...?"

"It is said that Polycarp shouted this at his back as he ran out: ... You fellow like the eldest son of Satan! ..."

Then a gentle wave of laughter arose among those present. Sosthenes spoke again.

"After that, Marcion is said to go around shouting this: all the disciples of Jesus who cling to the rotten law are disqualified, and only Paul is the true apostle."

Again the apostles all looked at the Apostle John at once. Still with his eyes half closed, John was nodding. Papias spoke again.

"Denying the law is a problem, but clinging to the law is also a problem. Such a problem has arisen in the church of Smyrna just north of here. These people rather cling to the law and say that they will keep purity for the coming of the Messiah. The Apostle has warned that they too are a synagogue of Satan."

Bishop Onesimus received those words.

"Wrong ideas about Christ seem to be spreading more every day. One of them is a man like Cerinthus, who is still lingering around Ephesus. He says that when Jesus was baptized, the spirit of Christ descended and came upon him, and then departed from him again when he died on the cross. If that is so, Christ's suffering and resurrection become completely meaningless."

Ignatius opened his mouth again. He looked at the flickering lamp and said,

"The characteristics of these mistaken people seem generally to be two. Because they despise the body, they either fall into extreme asceticism, or else they fall into extreme pleasure-seeking, saying that the body has nothing to do with salvation. Therefore, to run toward excessively fervent faith easily flows into something esoteric."

"The problem in the church of Thyatira is just such a thing. A woman named Jezebel is seducing fervent believers and drawing them into licentious esoteric rites."

"These truly are enemies more dreadful than Roman persecution. One day, when the Apostle John went to the bathhouse and learned that Cerinthus was there, he said there was no need to bathe in filthy water and came out of that place."

"And so..."

Ignatius again looked toward John and spoke as if resolved.

"The problems we have discussed until now... that is, the division of the church and the rise of heretical thoughts, are becoming enemies more dreadful than persecution and threats from outside. In order to guard the church from this crisis... I think the authority of the bishops must be strengthened above all."

Sosthenes, seated opposite Ignatius, received those words.

"That is so. I feel that the leadership of bishops who guide the brothers' faith onto the right path is necessary. But what we need above all seems to be the active intervention of the Apostle John, who met the Lord directly and accompanied him."

Onesimus, seated beside Sosthenes, also added to those words.

"Most of those who met the Lord directly have passed away, and even in this gathering, the only ones who met the Lord are the Apostle John and Bishop Ignatius. Therefore, in guarding the true image of the Lord, the testimony of you two is very important."

Still John sat quietly. From time to time he opened his half-closed eyes a little wider and nodded, but words did not easily flow from his mouth. The one who opened his mouth next was Papias of Hierapolis.

"... In order to guard the true image of Christ, I know that the disciples of Ephesus are collecting documents concerning the Lord. In Ephesus already, beginning with the Gospels of Mark and Matthew, Luke's Acts of the Apostles and many letters have been collected. But what I wish to suggest is that now John, the only apostle who remains, should personally leave a record that supplements the two Gospels."

The bishops' discussion now seemed to have reached its conclusion at last. When young Papias finished those words, all the bishops closed their mouths and watched only John's response.

The owl cried even more fiercely, and an excited spring wind was passing through the olive grove of Ortygia. John, who had been deep in thought for a while, slowly lifted his head and gazed silently at Papias's face.

"Papias... are you saying there is some problem with Mark's and Matthew's records?"

Papias kept his mouth closed for a moment, then spoke in a subdued voice.

"Of course I think there was nothing wrong in the records of those two. But what must be pointed out... is the fact that parts which naturally ought to have been recorded are missing."

"?"

"Anyone who heard the rumors concerning Jerusalem in the time of Jesus knows the resurrection of Lazarus, which was so very famous. Moreover, I heard that this event was the direct reason why the people of Jerusalem welcomed the Lord so passionately, and the fundamental motive that caused the Sanhedrin council to decide to remove Jesus. Yet for some reason, both Mark and Matthew keep silent about the matter of Lazarus."

Ignatius nodded and received those words.

"I too have examined Matthew's record in detail. I think the event in which the Lord withdrew to the region of Perea after the temple-blasphemy incident in which he raised Lazarus took place at the time of the Feast of Dedication, but the events of that time are not mentioned at all."

"That is so. The secret visit to Jerusalem at the Feast of Tabernacles, the problematic sermon at the Feast of Dedication, the withdrawal to Perea, and the events concerning Lazarus are all missing. And then it continues directly with the sermon concerning the little child, given in the region of Galilee."

A deep emotion passed over Ignatius's expression. Unlike his usual self, he spoke in a somewhat excited voice.

"From there is the part to which I can bear witness. It was precisely in a field near Capernaum. Hearing the rumor that Jesus had returned again to the region of Galilee, many people had gathered. I was barely six years old then. At the rumor that Jesus had come again, I gathered all the village children and went out to look at him. At that time, because there were so many people, we slipped between the legs of the adults and went forward. But the strange thing was..."

The other bishops all focused on Ignatius's mouth. It seemed as if a story of the background not recorded in the Gospels was about to emerge.

"The strange thing was that the apostles' expressions were very stiff, and I felt they were somewhat irritable. When we approached, they scolded us harshly as if venting their anger, and at last Jesus saw it, rebuked them, and then called me to come."

When the story reached a moving part, Ignatius stopped speaking for a moment and closed his eyes. Perhaps the gracious image of Jesus was now being revived before his eyes. After a moment, Ignatius slowly opened his eyes and opened his mouth.

"The Lord called me, held me in his arms, and continued speaking while stroking my head with his right hand. Unless you turn and become like children, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven... Therefore whoever humbles himself like this child is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven. Whoever causes one of these little ones to stumble, it would be better for him to have a millstone hung around his neck and to be drowned in the deep sea.... After saying that much, the Lord spoke words that were frightening even for the child I was to hear."

The bishops listened to his words with tension.

"If your hand or your foot causes you to sin, cut it off and throw it away. It is better for you to enter life maimed or lame than to have two hands and two feet and be thrown into eternal fire... If your eye causes you to sin, gouge it out and throw it away. It is better for you to enter life with one eye than to have two eyes and be thrown into the fire of hell. At that time, even with a child's heart, I could feel that something unusual was happening between Jesus and the disciples...."

The bishops all again looked at the Apostle John, who remained silent. John only continued nodding, and still made no comment. Sosthenes asked Ignatius.

"At that time... Bishop Ignatius, what kind of person did Jesus, whom you saw directly, appear to be?"

Following the lamplight, a faint flush passed over the old Ignatius's cheeks.

"That is truly... a wondrous thing. He clearly called me with a gesture, and he held me in his arms, yet strangely his appearance is not distinctly remembered. How shall I put it... he had no distinct features and had a very ordinary appearance. He had thick hair, an ordinary face, and eyes that looked at people warmly. His expression was gentle yet firm, and his body seemed thin yet ample."

"Did you, Lord Ignatius, continue following the Lord after that?"

"No. Because he went straight toward Jerusalem, we, who were too young, could no longer follow him."

Then the bishops' faces again turned toward Papias. This was because the part concerning the lost record had been connected again through Ignatius's testimony. Papias continued, looking toward John.

"What is stranger still... is that on the day Jesus arrived at Bethany, the entrance to Jerusalem, he ate supper at the house of a man named Simon the leper.

"As you know, Bethany is the village where Lazarus's family lived, and if Jesus arrived in Bethany after a long time, naturally a welcoming banquet should have been held at Lazarus's house. Yet Jesus went to Simon's house...."

If it was strange, then it was indeed a strange thing. Moreover, if he was Simon the leper, by the custom of Jerusalem he would have been isolated and housed in a place where lepers lived, and that house must probably have had its owner absent.

"And another point lacking explanation is this... On the day the Lord entered Jerusalem riding a young donkey, the people welcomed him so passionately that they took off their outer garments or broke off branches and spread them on the road, crying, Hosanna to the Son of David.... Yet five days later, they suddenly changed and cried out to crucify Jesus and kill him. What happened in between?"

Papias again watched John's expression for a moment, then continued.

"That is not all. Jesus, after entering Jerusalem and then coming into Bethany, went hungry the next morning. That morning, as Jesus left the village of Bethany, he was hungry and went under a fig tree, and when he saw that there was no fruit he could eat, he cursed that tree. The next day, when they looked, the tree had withered and died as he had cursed it. What indeed happened? Why did Jesus, who had been welcomed as the Messiah, and his disciples come to go hungry in the morning?"

As expected of Papias, who was skilled in writing, his questioning was sharp. When Papias's words ended, the bishops looked at John again.

Now it seemed that it was John's turn to open his mouth, and there was no avoiding it. He again let a pause linger, and then gazed steadily at Papias and said,

"Papias... you have studied many things."

"As you know, in our church of Hierapolis the four daughters of Philip the deacon are still living. Through them I was able to collect many stories handed down in the churches of Jerusalem and Caesarea."

John nodded again. As he remembered the figure of Philip the deacon, that passionate and active evangelist, tears were shining in his eyes. At last he began to open his mouth.

"It is shameful to say, but... neither I myself nor the other disciples knew who Jesus was or what relation he had to us. As you know, except for Simon the Zealot and Thaddaeus and Judas whom he brought, almost all of us were fishermen of Galilee. We simply followed because he called, and while seeing the many miracles he performed, we held simple desires that, if he who truly seemed to be the Savior should gain something great, perhaps we might also hold some office under him."

He breathed hard for a moment and looked down at the fluttering lamplight. John, the youngest and most ambitious of Jesus' disciples, the important figure who always accompanied Jesus when going to important places, was now speaking after a long time. A slight tension seemed even to hang among those present.

"If I confess honestly... we disciples also interpreted and judged him as we pleased, like the many heretics now. Thinking of it now, all error began there. When we think about it, what exactly is an idol of the Gentiles? Is it not that they draw and make the God they imagine in their own way? They all long for God and seek God, yet because they make God with their own wisdom and their own minds, it becomes an idol; and so the gods of all nations are each different, and gods and gods go to war with one another. But... the peculiar thing about the Hebrew people is... that they did not think of God's form in their own way, but drew it from God's standpoint. Thus Jehovah, the God of the Hebrew ancestors, became the One who is of himself. The Scriptures were not written by man; God called, and man wrote as his scribe. Look at Genesis. Everything could only be so. Since God is the master of heaven and earth, he could only create heaven and earth; and for that, he could only create light first; and since heaven and earth are a stage made for human beings, the human beings who would rule it could only be made in the likeness of God.... Apart from human conjecture, the work of God is proceeding. Christ... he could only come. He could only be born of a virgin, and because he was truth, he could only have power. Because he came as light in the midst of darkness, he could only suffer pain; but because truth cannot die, he could only live again; and in order to send the Holy Spirit, he could only ascend. All these things are being fulfilled apart from human conjecture...."

The old John's voice was overflowing with vitality, like that of a child. And his discourse, which pierced through the core, lodged in the bishops' hearts like a dagger.

"The problems of heresy we have discussed until now also arise, in the end, because people make God according to their own thoughts, just as the shamans of Gentile gods do. A person like Simon the magician, who followed Peter to the end and troubled him, was precisely of that kind. When Philip the deacon informed us of the great success of the evangelization of Samaria, Peter and I went there together to examine the condition of Samaria. They say that when Philip preached, there were many miracles of healing; and when Peter laid his hands on them, indeed the gifts of the Holy Spirit began to appear. At that time a magician named Simon, who had been baptized by Philip, offered money to Peter and begged him to teach him that technique. Simon in the end became a heretic, drifted all the way to Rome, and deceived many believers. Simon's problem was that he regarded the gift of the Holy Spirit as a human technique, and all heresies seem to be just like that. Especially, most of them seem to begin their misunderstanding from Greek knowledge and philosophy...."

John looked at Sosthenes of the Corinthian church and said,

"They seem to try to infer the relation between God and Christ from knowledge concerning nature and the universe. Yet after all, truth thought up by a human mind can only be dead truth. Rather than finding God through knowledge of the universe, would it not be the proper order to know God first, and from there learn the principles of the universe...?"

Papias, who had listened up to that point, realized that his suggestion was still valid and opened his mouth.

"Apostle, that is precisely it. We have received the mission to preserve the true image of Jesus, who is living truth, without damaging it. Unless you do this, Apostle...."

"Papias."

John looked at Papias with gentle eyes and said,

"The records of Mark and Matthew do not damage the Lord's image in the least. The two not only recorded his image accurately, but also the letters of the Apostle Paul...."

The bishops were all moved by John's generosity in calling Paul an apostle. Many people had disputed whether Paul had the qualification of an apostle or not, and Paul himself had felt this keenly, so that in each letter he called himself Paul, called to be an apostle of Christ Jesus according to the will of God. Yet now John was calling Paul an apostle as though it were only natural.

"The letters of the Apostle Paul testify with great accuracy to the meaning of the Lord's life, and if an insignificant fisherman now leaves behind a clumsy record, I fear it may instead become another deception. Papias, let us wait for God's will. For God is not one who leaves writings concerning himself to be made according to human beings' own will...."

"Then, Apostle."

Sosthenes, bishop of the Corinthian church, opened his mouth.

"Even if you leave the record of the Gospel to God's will... how would it be if you wrote letters from the Apostle to be sent to each church, in order to guide rightly the problems of fervor and heresy?"

John smiled gently and looked at Sosthenes.

"Already the Apostle Paul wrote this in his letter to the churches of Galatia: ... The fruit produced by the Holy Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, good deeds, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control. What do you think? With this one word, it seems it could become the best instruction to send to the Corinthian church."

"Apostle, it is the earnest request of all of us. On this occasion, please write one letter to circulate among the churches."

John could no longer hold out, and looked at Papias.

"Papias, do we now have papyrus and ink prepared?"

Papyrus referred to the scroll paper made by slicing and weaving the papyrus plant thinly. Since long ago, the people of this region had written important documents on papyrus scrolls with reeds and preserved them.

Papias opened the small door right beside him and went in, and soon came out carrying a bundle of papyrus, a jar of ink, and a bundle of reeds. Onesimus lifted the lamp that had been in the middle of the table and hung it on the lamp holder on the pillar between the walls. A scroll was spread upon the table, and Papias trimmed the end of a reed with a small knife. John closed his eyes and thought for a while, and then began to dictate a letter to the churches.

... We are going to speak concerning the Word of life. That Word existed from before heaven and earth were created. We heard that Word, saw it with our eyes, actually witnessed it, and touched it with our hands....

The people in the room were soaked in emotion as they watched the words Papias was writing down. What authoritative apostolic testimony it was. He was saying that he had heard that Word, seen it with his eyes, actually witnessed it, and touched it with his hands. It was truly a declaration like an explosion. It was one word that smashed at once the claims of all kinds of heresies.

... The purpose for which we proclaim to you what we have seen and heard is that you too may share the fellowship we have with the Father and with his Son Jesus Christ. We write this so that we may taste full joy.

... Dear children, I write this letter because your sins have been forgiven through Christ. Young people, I write this letter because you have already overcome the devil.

... Do not love the world or the things that belong to the world. In the person who loves the world, there is no love for the Father in his heart.

The apostle's letter continued for a long time. He stated that Christ is truth, and declared that any words that damage the truth are all enemies of Christ. And he said not to believe every word of people who say they have received the Holy Spirit, but to find out whether their claim truly comes from God. The standard for knowing this was precisely to acknowledge that Jesus Christ, the Son of God, came in the body of a man; anyone who denies this is not a person who has received the Holy Spirit.

He also said this: the way to reject words of deception is only to rely on the help of the Holy Spirit, and to do so, they must love one another. Since all heresy comes from strife, the counsel agapate allelous, love one another, flowed pulse by pulse through the whole letter.

... Beloved, I urge you. Let us love one another. Love comes from God. Everyone who loves has been born of God and knows God. Whoever does not love does not know God, because God is love.

Since in this world we have come to live like Christ, it is clear that love has been completed in us. Now we have come to face the day of judgment with confidence. There is no fear in love. Perfect love drives out fear.

The image of John, who had once been quick-tempered and rough, was nowhere to be seen; only the generous and quiet counsel of the old apostle overflowed in the letter. Whenever black letters filled the papyrus scroll, the scent of John, who had thoroughly endured, spread like fragrance.

Ever since meeting the risen Jesus on the shore of Tiberias, John had never once stepped forward before others. In the Jerusalem church he put Peter forward, and even when visiting the churches he was always in the background. He always followed behind Peter like a shadow, and worked from behind. Only after receiving Paul's request had he moved to the Antioch church where Paul was absent, and after Paul died and even his successor Timothy was martyred, he had again moved to Ephesus. John, who had been like a ball of fire, had now changed into a person calm as clear water.

But in truth, those long years had been hard for John to endure. When all his companions were martyred, shed blood, and fell, he could not obtain that opportunity. Because of the teacher's last request, the one word entrusting his mother Mary to him, he had to remain all along outside the line of martyrdom and avoid danger. Whenever the hot blood of youth boiled up, John had to revive that dreadful memory from the shore of Tiberias and chew it down and swallow it.

John's long silence had begun precisely from the event at Tiberias. But now, at last, the old apostle who had lived a life of silence was beginning, little by little, to open his mouth.

When Papias finished writing and folded the scroll carefully, suddenly someone was knocking from outside on the locked door.

All the people in the room tensed and looked toward the door. Since the emperor's persecution was growing more and more severe, and since this hut in Ortygia was now a place where important bishops of the church had gathered, they could not help being tense. Onesimus sprang up from his seat and spoke toward the outside.

"... Who is it?"

"It is I. Polycarp."

Only after hearing Polycarp's familiar voice did Onesimus stroke his chest in relief. When he removed the door bar, three men entered the narrow room. Onesimus was startled once more and opened his eyes wide.

"Why, who is this? Is this not Teacher Tychicus?"

"Onesimus...."

The two old men embraced each other and for a while did not know what to do.

"How long has it been? ... Onesimus, you too have grown very old."

"Teacher, where have you been all this time? I thought for certain that you, who had gone into Rome, had been martyred."

"Now, Onesimus... besides us, there seem to be several others in this room, so first let me greet them."

"That is so. I was so overwhelmed that I..."

Onesimus first introduced Tychicus to John across from him.

"Apostle, this is Teacher Tychicus, famous for having swift feet."

Through Onesimus's introduction, Tychicus realized that the old man across from him was the Apostle John. He took one step forward and bowed his head deeply.

"Because this Tychicus has lived long, I receive the honor of seeing the Apostle."

John also rose from his seat, extended his hand, and took Tychicus's rough hand.

"I have heard much about you from Onesimus. That you went with him as far as Colossae in order to have Onesimus's sin forgiven...."

Onesimus added an explanation beside him.

"That is so. When I was a slave in the house of Philemon of the Colossian church, I stole his goods and fled, but in prison in Rome I met Paul, received Christ, and was baptized. Paul wrote a letter to Philemon for the forgiveness of my sin, but because he was concerned that Philemon might be angry, he had Teacher Tychicus accompany me. Thanks to that, I received forgiveness for my sin, became a free man, and thus became a servant who serves the church."

Onesimus introduced Tychicus again to the other bishops. Since the bishops too already remembered Tychicus's name from the Acts of the Apostles and Paul's letters, they exchanged greetings as if they had met before. Meanwhile, Polycarp and Prochorus also gladly greeted their friend Papias, and were introduced to Bishops Ignatius and Sosthenes as well.

Soon they sat around, offered a prayer of thanks to God who had protected them until this day, and then looked at one another, having so much to say that they could not find where to begin. Those who met Bishop Ignatius for the first time looked with wonder at the figure of one who had directly received Jesus' blessing; Tychicus was overwhelmed as he looked at the Apostle John; and those seeing Tychicus for the first time wanted to hear about his past years.

John asked Polycarp, who was seated beside him, in a small voice.

"Did Callimachus not come with you?"

"No... he had something to see to in Ephesus, so he will probably come to the dawn worship tomorrow."

"Probably...?"

Since it was natural for a believer to come to worship, John asked in puzzlement because of Polycarp's expression. Polycarp quickly corrected his answer.

"He said he would come to the dawn worship."

When those present had grown quiet for a moment, Onesimus said,

"Since many are meeting for the first time, there must be much that each wants to say and hear, but Teacher Tychicus, who has just arrived, has come to Ephesus after thirty years, so how would it be if we first heard some of his story?"

Then Tychicus set down the fish he had been holding in his hand and the bundle he had been carrying on his back, and spoke with a somewhat embarrassed expression.

"As Brother Onesimus told you earlier, I visited the church of Colossae carrying Paul's letters to Philemon of the Colossian church and to other believers. That was just before the great fire of Rome. As Paul had asked, I met Philemon in the Colossian church and begged forgiveness for Brother Onesimus, and then returned to this Ephesus. At that time, I thought a great persecution must have arisen in the Ephesian church and that many people had been martyred, but unexpectedly an apostasy had taken place. It was the time when, led by Onesiphorus, Phygelus, Hermogenes, and others whom Paul had trusted completely, all the saints had apostatized and the church had been scattered to the winds. I was so shocked that I immediately ran to Rome and reported this fact to Paul. Not long after that, the great fire of Rome broke out, and the brothers in Rome began to be persecuted again. Paul was arrested again, and from prison he wrote a letter to the Ephesian believers, gave it to me, and told me to go once more into Ephesus, find the scattered believers, and convey his request. I escaped from Rome with Paul's letter, but I was caught because of the accusation of a Jew who knew my face. Taken back to Rome, I came to be sent into a gladiatorial contest with wild beasts in the Circus of Vaticanus."

Everyone in the room listened to Tychicus's recollection with astonishment. How many brothers had been torn to death by the blades of gladiators and the teeth of wild beasts in the arenas of Rome! And now Tychicus, who sat before their eyes, was saying that he had gone into that arena.

"In the arena there were two lions, two leopards, and eight gladiators. But though they were called gladiators, we were all Christiani who had undergone only brief training, so it was no different from going out simply to die. I thought only of the cross of Christ and swung net and sword toward the wild beasts that charged. The eight of us miraculously succeeded in covering one lion and one leopard with nets, but during that time I alone, because I was swift-footed, survived, and all the other believers were bitten to death by the beasts."

Tychicus's eyes still flashed as if the memory of that time were vivid, and those listening swallowed dryly.

"At that moment the crowd's roar burst out, so I looked to see what was happening, and a young officer was charging in on a chariot. It was Acilius Glabrio, who had made his name in the gladiatorial arena with astonishing skill. As soon as he entered, he leveled his spear and charged toward one lion. But at that very moment, the remaining leopard leaped right up onto the chariot and lunged toward the back of Glabrio's neck. Startled, I threw the sword I was holding, without even realizing it, toward the leopard's back. The crowd screamed, and when the leopard stabbed in the back attacked me, I threw the net again. Only after dealing with the lion before him did Glabrio realize that he himself had been in danger, and he petitioned Nero, who was seated in the emperor's place, to spare my life."

After that, two choices were given to Tychicus: to continue the life of a gladiator, or to go to the mines of Hispania. Choosing to become a slave in the mines rather than a gladiator who would someday have to kill a person, he departed for the mines of Hispania. To Glabrio, who came to see him, Tychicus entrusted Paul's scroll, saying that someday he would come back to retrieve it.

Thus Tychicus spent thirty-five years in the mines. In Hispania, where Paul had always wanted to go, Tychicus preached the gospel of Christ diligently, though it was to the slaves of the mines. Then one day, Tychicus suddenly received release after thirty years.

Tychicus, who went to Glabrio's house just in case, learned from the people of that house that Glabrio had been arrested. Amazingly, it was said he had been arrested on suspicion of being a Christianus. His old steward unexpectedly handed Paul's letter to Tychicus and said this: Glabrio, who had left the scroll thrown into a storage room for thirty years, one day happened to enter the storeroom and discovered it. Reading down the scroll out of curiosity, he gradually became interested, and at last came even to attend their secret meetings in disguise. Glabrio, who had finally become a fervent Christianus, came to think of Tychicus, who had entrusted Paul's scroll to him, as the benefactor of his whole life. Recalling the memory from thirty years earlier and asking around, he found him and carried out the procedure for his release. Since he had already risen to the consulship and retired, his power was still great, so releasing one slave was an easy thing. But not long afterward, Glabrio was accused by an informer and arrested. As he was taken away under arrest, he asked the old steward to hand that scroll over if Tychicus came.

Only then did Tychicus begin to untie the bundle he had set down. From the bundle wrapped layer upon layer, at last an old papyrus scroll was revealed. Tychicus carefully lifted it and held it out before John.

"Paul's letter to the Ephesian believers has now arrived after thirty years."

"Oh...."

John's hands trembled faintly as he received the scroll.

Paul's final letter, sent to the church of Ephesus after finishing a perilous voyage of faithfulness and awaiting martyrdom, had come. It was a fearsome writing that had endured for as many as thirty years and made the former Roman consul Acilius Glabrio a Christianus. His Excellency Glabrio, the hero of the gladiatorial games who had swept through the arena together with wild beasts and received the crowd's frenzy upon himself, had knelt before the cross of Christ. Perhaps many saints had been pierced by his sword and died. That the persecutor smelling of blood had been born again after reading Paul's letter, and had become a worker of Christ, was truly a strange thing.

John untied the cord of the scroll with trembling hands. When the scroll opened, letters written as if carved into it appeared there. And in places among those letters there were stains. They might have been the marks of the thick tears of repentance shed by Glabrio, the foremost warrior of Rome. John slowly began to read Paul's letter.

I, Paul, who became an apostle of Christ Jesus according to the will of God, write this letter to the saints in Ephesus who believe faithfully in Christ Jesus. May God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ give you grace and peace....

Though it had arrived after thirty years, Paul's hot heart thinking of the brothers of Ephesus overflowed on the papyrus paper.

... In order to make us live together with Christ, before the creation of heaven and earth he had already chosen us, and through his love made us holy and blameless, so that we could stand before him. God determined beforehand, through Jesus Christ, to make us his children. This was what God willed and delighted in. For this glorious grace, which he freely gave us through his beloved Son, we cannot help praising God....

Onesimus wiped tears with the back of his hand, and tears gathered in the eyes of John and the other bishops as well. Tychicus, with his eyes closed, was listening to Paul's voice coming through John.

... You too were once people dead because of sins and wrongdoings. When you were bound by sin, you lived according to the custom of this world, lived as directed by the ruler of the power that governs the air, and lived as directed by the evil spirit that today controls those who disobey God. In truth, we too were all like them; formerly, we lived according to instinctive desires, drawn by passions, and by nature, like other people, could only incur the wrath of God. But God, who is infinitely merciful, loved us with his great love, and made us who had done wrong and were dead live again together with Christ. Now you are no longer foreigners or travelers. You are fellow citizens with the saints and one family of God. If you are a building, Christ is the most essential cornerstone of that building, and the apostles and prophets are its foundation. Every building is joined together around this cornerstone and grows greater and greater, becoming the Lord's holy temple. You also are being built together around this cornerstone and becoming a spiritual house of God....

The stubborn and self-righteous personality of Paul was completely gone within this letter. What was more astonishing was that this was a tearful appeal to the church of Ephesus, where everyone had apostatized and been scattered to the winds, asking them to be joined and built together around Christ and become a temple.

Paul then urged the Ephesians not to be shaken by temptation, but to forgive one another and become one. He told them not to grieve the Holy Spirit, but to discern what pleases the Lord, and to pledge victory in an evil age.

... Strive to preserve what the Holy Spirit has given you, binding you together as one with the bond of peace. The body of Christ is one, and the Spirit is one. Likewise, the hope God gives you when he calls you to be his people is one. The Lord is one, faith is one, baptism is one, and God, the Father of all people, is one.

The old John's voice was growing more and more resonant. Though the lamplight was not bright, the room was full of hot emotion. They were all now meeting Paul. Ignatius was meeting again Paul, who had pioneered the Antioch church together with Barnabas; Sosthenes was meeting again Paul, who had been seized and tried in Corinth; Onesimus was meeting again Paul, whom he had met in prison in Rome, and who had urged him to return to Philemon and ask forgiveness; and Tychicus was meeting again Paul's last figure, when he had written this very letter and prayed for him. Not only that, but young Papias, Polycarp, and Prochorus too were placing their hands on their chests, thinking of Paul's final martyrdom, when he had been beheaded in Rome.

At last John reached Paul's final greeting.

I want you also to know the circumstances I am now in and the work I am doing. Our dear brother Tychicus will tell you all this news. He is a faithful worker who believes in the Lord. I am sending this man to you in order to let you know our circumstances and to encourage your hearts. May God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ give peace to the brothers, and love together with faith. Also, may grace be upon all who love our Lord Jesus Christ without change.

Paul's long letter ended there. Even after the letter ended, John held the papyrus scroll for a long while and looked down at it, and everyone seemed still to be listening for Paul's appeal, as if there were yet more.

Tychicus began to explain Paul's last appearance in a low voice.

"When Paul was imprisoned the second time... he seemed already to know that the time was near. His appearance was very haggard, but his eyes flashed like those of a triumphant general. Perhaps because he was aware it was the last, Paul greatly wanted to see people. Those who had followed him had all been scattered, and only Luke was caring for him. He especially wanted to see Bishop Timothy, and he also wanted to meet Mark, whom he had rebuked and parted from when setting out on the second missionary journey, but in the end it seems he met no one and faced his last moment."

Paul's lonely end hurt their hearts all the more. After silence had flowed for quite some time, John asked Tychicus again.

"How are the believers of the Roman church doing?"

"The activities of the Roman believers, which had been tolerated to some extent during Emperor Titus's time, fell headlong back into darkness when Emperor Domitian ascended the throne. During that time the gospel in Rome had brought about an enormous work, so that, as I said earlier, even former consul Acilius Glabrio became a Christianus; but now the bloody persecution has resumed. Moreover, recently one more important person among the secret believers in the Roman church has been arrested."

All the people present opened their eyes round and looked at Tychicus. They could not hide their surprise that there was someone even greater than the former consul Acilius Glabrio. John leaned his upper body forward and pressed him with a question.

"Who is that?"

"It is His Excellency the consul Flavius Clemens."

Everyone was startled and looked at one another. From Bishop Ignatius's mouth, like a groan,

"Hallelujah!"

an exclamation of praise escaped. Truly, Jesus, who had been executed in a shabby form outside the gate of Jerusalem, had brought to his knees Clemens, consul of Rome and nephew of the emperor.

"Lady Flavia Domitilla, the wife of His Excellency Clemens, was the greatest supporter of the Roman church. Beneath the land belonging to the house of Domitilla at Ardeatina, there is also a catacomb, a gathering place of the Roman church."

"Then... are you saying Sister Domitilla has also been arrested?"

"For now only His Excellency Clemens has been arrested, but I have a premonition that before long disaster will reach her as well...."

"Though Titus was an enemy who trampled Jerusalem, it is said that in his time the good works of the Roman church even received some praise from the emperor... What do they say is the cause for Domitian's resumption of persecution?"

"... Sadly, it is said to be because of the accusations of the Jews."

"The Jews...."

"That is so. In order to protect the synagogue recognized by Rome, they are making the Christiani into scapegoats. The Jews wish to gain the emperor's favor by accusing the Christiani of being a secret society engaged in an anti-government movement, that is, a collegia. Not long ago the Lord's relatives living in Rome... that is, the sons and grandsons of Judah, were imprisoned because of a Jew's accusation."

Judah was Jesus' youngest brother. Jesus had three younger brothers, James, Simon, and Judah. James led the Jerusalem church and was martyred in Jerusalem two years before Paul; Simon was martyred in Persia; and Judah was martyred in Rome.

It was precisely Judah's descendants who had been staying near Rome. The night was growing deeper and deeper. On this gloomy night when the owls cried so, the spring wind passed through the olive grove with a sharp sound.
