# Empire and Heaven (Volume I)
## Chapter 3 — Through the Darkness

Even after the red sun disappeared beneath the Mediterranean, the stone steps of the great theater still held traces of fading light.

Along the broad avenue stretching toward the harbor, lamps slowly awakened one by one beneath the deepening dusk.

Far below, fireworks still burst above the harbor district where the Festival of Artemis continued through the night.

Halfway up the theater steps, three young men stood watching the avenue in silence.

At last the taller of the three turned toward the handsome young man beside him.

"Callimachus... I wish there were some way I could help you."

Callimachus stared toward the harbor lights shimmering in the distance.

A cold smile touched his lips.

"Do not trouble yourself so much, Polycarp. I am ashamed to appear this weak before you again. Since childhood, you have always been the one offering comfort... while I have always been the one receiving it."

Polycarp answered quietly.

"But we live by hope, Callimachus. I still believe that we are strongest when we are weakest."

Silence settled over them once more.

Then the broad-shouldered youth with curly hair suddenly pointed down toward the avenue.

"I think he is coming this way."

All three turned their eyes toward the marble road below.

An old man carrying a fish in one hand was climbing steadily toward the theater.

Polycarp slowly nodded.

"Then our suspicions were correct."

The old man moved much faster now than he had while wandering through the city.

He crossed the Ephesian square, passed over the marble roadway, and entered the theater itself.

Polycarp spoke softly.

"We should not make the elder walk any farther. Let us go down to meet him ourselves."

The three young men descended the steps together.

In the growing darkness, the distance between them and the old traveler slowly disappeared.

The old man passed beyond the stage building and entered the open semicircular court where musicians and choruses usually gathered before performances.

At last they stood face to face.

For a moment no one spoke.

Then Polycarp stepped forward.

"The fish you carry... what does it mean?"

The old man studied Polycarp quietly before answering.

"Ichthys."

The Greek word for fish.

Polycarp immediately asked another question.

"Do you know the meaning of the Ichthys?"

The old man slowly spoke the ancient confession.

"Iesous Christos Theou Huios Soter."

Jesus Christ, Son of God, Savior.

The secret confession shared among believers.

The moment the words left his mouth, Polycarp seized the old man's thin hands with emotion.

"Welcome, brother. My name is Polycarp. This is my friend Callimachus... and this is Prochorus. We serve among the believers here in Ephesus."

The old man looked at them carefully.

"It has been many years since I left this city. I no longer know the names of the young. Were your fathers believers also?"

Polycarp nodded.

"Prochorus is the grandson of the elder Prochorus, one of the first seven servants of the Jerusalem church. He later shepherded the church in Nicomedia before his death."

Then Polycarp rested a hand gently upon Callimachus' shoulder.

"And Callimachus' father was once a devoted follower of Christ."

The old man looked carefully at Callimachus.

"Once devoted?"

An awkward silence followed.

Before Polycarp could answer, the old man asked quietly:

"What was your father's name?"

"Onesiphorus," Callimachus replied.

At that name, something changed in the old man's expression.

"Onesiphorus..."

The name escaped his lips almost like a prayer.

"A faithful man," he murmured. "Where is he now?"

"He died before I was born," Callimachus answered softly.

The old man lowered his head.

"Yes... Onesiphorus was a faithful man indeed."

Seeing sorrow darken Callimachus' face, Polycarp quickly changed the subject.

"Who is it that you seek in Ephesus, elder?"

The old traveler slowly lifted his eyes.

"I have returned to Ephesus after thirty years. Can you guide me to the brothers? Who now shepherds the church here?"

"Bishop Onesimus," Polycarp answered.

At once the old man's voice trembled.

"Onesimus..."

His eyes glistened in the darkness.

"The same Onesimus who once came from Colossae?"

"You truly know him," Polycarp said in surprise. "After the martyrdom of the apostle Paul, he eventually returned to Ephesus."

The old man closed his eyes briefly.

"So he still lives..."

The young men exchanged startled glances.

Polycarp spoke carefully.

"Forgive us, elder... but who exactly are you?"

The old traveler smiled faintly.

"I knew Timothy. I knew Onesiphorus. And long ago, I traveled beside Paul himself."

The three young men froze.

Then Polycarp whispered almost breathlessly:

"Are you... Tychicus?"

The old man gave a weary nod.

"Yes."

Shock swept across their faces.

For years the believers of Ephesus had preserved copies of Paul's letters.

The name Tychicus appeared within them like a distant figure from sacred memory.

Polycarp spoke rapidly.

"You carried Paul's letters with Onesimus to the churches! We still read them among the believers!"

Tychicus lowered his eyes.

"So many years have passed..."

"The churches have waited for your coming," Polycarp said.

A faint smile appeared on the old man's face.

"Then my journey only arrived thirty years late."

Prochorus stepped closer eagerly.

"Tell us... when did you first meet the apostle Paul?"

And there, beneath the growing darkness of the Ephesian theater, Tychicus began speaking of the past.

Of Paul arriving in Ephesus.

Of Aquila and Priscilla.

Of Timothy.

Of the hall of Tyrannus where the apostle taught daily.

Of the sick being healed.

Of demons cast out.

Of magicians burning their books in fear.

And finally, of the riot.

The roar of craftsmen furious that the name of Artemis was losing power over the people.

The madness of the crowds filling this very theater.

"Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!"

Even now Tychicus seemed able to hear their screams echoing through the stone steps.

The old man pointed toward the harbor road below.

"They shouted not only for their goddess... but for their livelihood. Fear ruled them more than devotion ever did."

Far below, the festival procession of Artemis once again appeared beneath the torchlight.

Music thundered.

Fireworks exploded.

The goddess moved proudly through the avenue while crowds celebrated beneath the night sky.

For a long moment, none of the four men spoke.

The light of Artemis flickered across their faces.

Then Polycarp quietly asked:

"And Paul? Did he survive the riot?"

Tychicus gave a faint laugh.

"Barely. He wanted to enter the theater himself and face the mob. We had to stop him."

A small smile touched his face at the memory.

"The one who conceived the plan to hide him safely was your father, Callimachus. Onesiphorus saved Paul's life that night."

Callimachus lowered his head.

The noise of the city drifted upward around them.

Torchlight.
Music.
Shouting.
The smell of smoke.

And above it all, the stars slowly appeared over Ephesus.

Polycarp spoke again.

"Come with us, elder. We will take you to Bishop Onesimus."

Tychicus picked up the fish he had dropped earlier.

"Then lead the way, brothers."

Together they turned away from the theater and disappeared into the growing darkness beyond the city lights.

As they descended the marble streets of Ephesus, the sounds of celebration echoed endlessly around them.

Yet beneath the noise of the city, another current seemed to move unseen.

Quiet.
Hidden.
Persistent.

The kingdom of Rome filled the streets with power.

But another kingdom moved silently through homes, prisons, harbors, and forgotten corners of the Empire.

And though few yet understood it, the collision between those two kingdoms had already begun.
