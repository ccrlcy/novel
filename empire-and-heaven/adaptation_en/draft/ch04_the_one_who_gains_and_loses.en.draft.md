# Empire and Heaven (Volume I)
## Chapter 4 — The One Who Gains and the One Who Loses

Seeing lights still burning inside the Hall of Tyrannus, Andronicus tilted his head slightly as he dismounted from his horse.

At this hour, the students should already have returned home.

And with the Festival of Artemis filling the city streets, anyone remaining in Ephesus would surely have gone to join the celebrations.

The academy stood along the roadside as a graceful two-story building. Passing through its marble archway, one entered a small garden before reaching the inner halls.

As Andronicus stepped through the gate, he paused.

Children's voices drifted softly through the evening air.

Among them, from time to time, came the voice of a woman.

And beneath it all lingered the warm scent of food.

Slowly he moved toward the sound.

Inside one of the open halls, dozens of children sat receiving food from a woman standing among them.

The children wore ragged clothes.

Their faces and hands were stained with dirt from the streets.

Instinctively, Andronicus frowned.

Even the glory of Rome could not erase the poverty multiplying through every city like disease.

No matter how much grain was distributed, beggars and abandoned children always returned.

They were the hidden shame beneath the splendor of the Empire.

Roman nobles had long since learned to look away.

But Andronicus could not.

Something about the woman distributing the food seemed strangely familiar.

He stepped closer.

Then her voice reached him clearly.

"Everyone has received food now, yes? Then let us thank God who has provided for us tonight. Who would like to pray for us today?"

A boy sitting in the front row immediately sprang to his feet.

Unlike the others, his clothes were relatively clean.

His eyes shone brightly.

"Then our good brother Gaius will pray for us today. Fold your hands and close your eyes."

The noisy children instantly fell silent.

The boy bowed his head.

"Our loving Father in heaven, thank You for giving us our daily bread once again today. Help us grow healthy and strong so that we may live with truth and learn to love our neighbors. In the name of our Lord Jesus Christ we pray."

"Amen," the children answered together.

The moment they opened their eyes, they froze.

All of them were staring toward the doorway.

The woman turned as well.

Her eyes widened.

Though she wore a plain and worn stola, though loose strands of hair clung to her forehead dampened with sweat, Andronicus recognized her immediately.

The bright eyes.

The graceful mouth.

The same warmth he had remembered for six long years.

He swallowed slowly.

"Drusiana... do you recognize me? I am Marius Andronicus."

A gentle smile slowly appeared upon her lips.

"I recognized you at once," she answered softly. "You are still handsome... and still carry yourself like a Roman officer."

Then she glanced down at her own simple clothing and laughed quietly.

"Had I known you were coming tonight, I might have tried to look more beautiful."

"Impossible," Andronicus replied. "Drusiana has always been beautiful."

Suddenly aware that all the children were staring at them, Drusiana turned back toward the room.

"Finish your meals, everyone. And remember to gather the bowls together afterward like always."

Then she led Andronicus outside into the courtyard.

Yellow narcissus flowers shimmered beside the fountain beneath the evening light.

"It has been a long time since your return to Ephesus," she said. "We heard many stories from travelers coming from Rome."

"I was afraid," Andronicus admitted quietly. "We were still children when we last met. I wondered whether we would even know how to speak to one another now."

Drusiana smiled.

"You were always very shy when we were young. Whenever girls greeted you on the streets, your face would turn red."

"Was I truly that awkward?"

"You were famous among the girls of Ephesus."

For the first time that evening, Andronicus laughed.

Then his expression softened.

"Drusiana... do you remember the night we first danced together? During the Festival of Artemis?"

"Of course," she answered. "All the young men and women wore masks that night."

"We danced together for hours without knowing who the other was."

Drusiana looked up at him with amused eyes.

"I knew who you were from the beginning."

"How?"

"Because even from your footsteps alone... I could recognize you."

Silence lingered gently between them.

Then Drusiana looked toward the academy building.

"How did you come here tonight? Was this an official visit? Should I bring you inside to meet the others?"

Andronicus hesitated.

"Before that... there is something I wished to say to you first."

Her expression grew still.

"I asked Master Apollonius for permission to seek your hand in marriage."

Drusiana lowered her eyes quietly.

"Before speaking with your family, I wished to hear your own thoughts first."

"Why would a man like you still think of me after living in Rome?" she asked softly. "Surely there are many noble and beautiful women there."

"Drusiana," he replied, "after leaving Ephesus six years ago, I never forgot you even once. I never imagined another woman as my wife."

Her eyes trembled slightly.

"And perhaps," he continued, "I hoped that at least a small part of your heart still remembered me as well."

"Marius..."

She brushed back a loose strand of hair.

"Every young woman in Ephesus has imagined you as a husband at least once. And I... perhaps more often than most. But you belong to Rome now. You are a man the Empire itself expects great things from."

"None of that matters to me. If you wished it, I could abandon every office Rome offered me."

He stepped closer.

"Nothing stands between us except your answer."

Slowly he reached for her hand.

"Say yes, Drusiana. And tonight I will feel more victorious than any conqueror returning from war."

For a moment she allowed her hand to remain within his.

Then gently she withdrew it.

"You spoke with Master Apollonius already?"

"Yes."

"Then he must have told you about my faith."

"Your Christianity?"

She nodded.

"Rome tolerates many religions," Andronicus answered carefully. "What you believe does not matter to me."

Drusiana looked at him sadly.

"But it matters to Rome. Christians are no longer merely misunderstood. They are becoming enemies of the Empire."

Andronicus frowned.

"The government blames Christians for things they did not do. Even now in Rome, men are imprisoned under accusations that have little to do with religion itself."

"Exactly," Drusiana whispered. "Christians are called dangerous not because they seek power... but because they refuse to worship power."

For a moment neither spoke.

Andronicus thought again of Flavius Clemens.

A consul.
A nobleman.
A relative of the emperor himself.

Even he had fallen beneath suspicion.

To marry a Christian woman now was no small matter.

Yet Andronicus lifted his head stubbornly.

"I am not coward enough to marry a woman I do not love simply because Rome disapproves. If something must be sacrificed for you, then let it be sacrificed."

He stepped closer once more.

"That is how a young man ought to live."

Drusiana looked at him with trembling eyes.

"And if we were married," she whispered, "could you stand beside me at the Eucharist? Could you receive the bread and wine of Christ? Could you receive the blessing of the apostle himself?"

"The apostle John?"

"Yes."

Andronicus answered without hesitation.

"If it means remaining beside you, I would do anything."

Their faces slowly drew closer.

Then suddenly a voice came from the darkness beyond the garden.

"Your courage is admirable, Quaestor Andronicus."

Instantly Andronicus turned.

A pale figure emerged slowly from the shadows.

"Who dares listen secretly to another man's conversation?"

The young man laughed quietly.

"Why so alarmed? You look as though an imperial informer has appeared before you."

Drusiana covered her mouth softly.

"Callimachus..."

The young man stopped beneath the garden torchlight.

"Forgive me for interrupting," he said. "But it seemed time that certain matters finally be settled."
