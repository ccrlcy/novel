# Empire and Heaven — Master Prose Direction Map

Status: active production guide  
Scope: Volume I and Volume II English literary adaptation  
Purpose: define chapter-by-chapter prose pressure, emotional temperature, thematic function, and adaptation constraints before final English prose overwrite.

---

## Global Adaptation Standard

The English adaptation must read as biblical historical literary fiction, not literal translation.

Core prose identity:

- restrained
- cinematic
- spiritually weighted
- emotionally controlled
- low-exposition where possible
- atmospheric rather than explanatory
- modern readable English with biblical gravity

Core theological spine:

- Kingdom of God vs Empire
- visible power vs hidden truth
- allegiance before desire
- endurance under pressure
- faithfulness without sensationalism
- sacrifice without melodrama

Core style rule:

> Less explanation. More presence. More inevitability.

Forbidden tendencies:

- translationese
- sermonized narration
- exaggerated martyr rhetoric
- cartoon evil Rome
- sentimental romance excess
- modern slang
- overexplained historical lectures

Preferred cadence:

- scene-led exposition
- short weighted dialogue
- silence as dramatic pressure
- sensory restraint
- emotional subtext
- chapter endings that linger rather than announce

# Volume I — Upper Volume

## Chapter 1 — The Encroaching Wind

Source title: 스며드는 바람

### Narrative Function
Opening sea chapter. Establishes Andronicus, Hesthenes, Ephesus memory, Rome’s grandeur, and the first invisible tremor beneath imperial certainty.

### Prose Direction
The prose should feel clear, blue, wind-borne, and quietly unsettled. The surface is bright Mediterranean travel; the underside is the first hint that Rome is not eternal.

### Emotional Temperature
Calm, nostalgic, uneasy.

### Thematic Pressure
Visible empire vs invisible approaching truth.

### Character Focus
- Andronicus: young, ambitious, already tired beneath success.
- Hesthenes: sharp, loyal, ironic, observant.
- Tychicus: quiet spiritual gravity; never theatrical.

### Motifs
Sea, wind, sail, silence, Rome, memory, invisible decay.

### Ending Resonance
A storm has not yet arrived, but the air has changed.

### Adaptation Cautions
Compress historical exposition into atmosphere. Do not over-explain Ephesus. Preserve longing for Drusiana but avoid melodrama.

## Chapter 2 — Strangers

Source title: 낯선 사람들

### Narrative Function
Arrival in Ephesus. The city appears dazzling, wealthy, and religiously intoxicated. Artemis spectacle and imperial worship establish a civilization worshiping power, pleasure, and fear.

### Prose Direction
Crowded, torchlit, ritual-heavy. The city must feel alive but not at peace.

### Emotional Temperature
Magnificent, feverish, spiritually hollow.

### Thematic Pressure
Civilization at its height quietly losing its soul.

### Character Focus
- Andronicus: recognizes beauty but senses dislocation.
- Hesthenes: perceives social decay beneath civic order.
- Tychicus: background gravity, moving quietly through spectacle.

### Motifs
Incense, blood, procession, marble, torches, crowd noise, emperor image.

### Ending Resonance
Ephesus shines, but hidden cracks have begun to show.

### Adaptation Cautions
Avoid tourist-guide description. Artemis ritual should feel like collective spiritual fever, not decorative pagan scenery.

---

## Chapter 3 — Through the Darkness

Source title: 어둠을 헤치고

### Narrative Function
Hidden believers meet Tychicus. Apostolic memory enters the present. The church is small, quiet, and historically fragile, yet spiritually continuous.

### Prose Direction
Night-theater stillness. The loud city remains below; hidden faith breathes above it.

### Emotional Temperature
Quiet, reverent, restrained, memory-laden.

### Thematic Pressure
The Empire fills history loudly. Truth survives quietly through those who remain faithful.

### Character Focus
- Polycarp: young shepherd warmth, faithful but not yet legendary.
- Callimachus: wounded, brilliant, spiritually unsettled.
- Prochorus: earnest witness and continuity figure.
- Tychicus: living memory of the apostolic age.

### Motifs
Darkness, fish, theater stone, hidden recognition, remembered names.

### Ending Resonance
The apostolic age is fading, but not extinguished.

### Adaptation Cautions
Do not turn Tychicus into a grand hero. He is a tired, faithful witness who kept walking.

---

## Chapter 4 — The One Who Gains and the One Who Loses

Source title: 얻는 자와 잃은 자

### Narrative Function
Andronicus and Drusiana meet again. Romantic longing becomes a spiritual crossroads. Callimachus enters as unresolved fracture.

### Prose Direction
Tender but restrained. The chapter begins in warmth and ends with the first real pressure of competing allegiances.

### Emotional Temperature
Tender, divided, quietly sorrowful.

### Thematic Pressure
Love remains sincere, but truth begins separating worlds.

### Character Focus
- Andronicus: believes love can reconcile Rome and faith.
- Drusiana: warm, steady, already belonging to another kingdom.
- Callimachus: rival, wounded believer, mirror of unresolved fracture.

### Motifs
Food, children, garden, hand, Eucharist, interruption.

### Ending Resonance
No one is trying to harm the other, yet truth itself is creating distance.

### Adaptation Cautions
Avoid modern romance cadence. Drusiana should not sound argumentative; she should sound spiritually clear.

---

## Chapter 5 — The Midnight Watch

Source title: 심야의 파수대

### Narrative Function
Church leadership wrestles with internal crisis, delayed return, doctrinal strain, persecution, and unity. The danger is not only Rome outside but fragmentation inside.

### Prose Direction
Interior, lamplit, anxious but disciplined. A council chamber under pressure.

### Emotional Temperature
Grave, watchful, intellectually tense.

### Thematic Pressure
Truth must endure not only persecution but confusion.

### Character Focus
- John: aged authority, restrained but piercing.
- Ignatius, Onesimus, Papias, Sosthenes: varied church voices under strain.
- Young leaders: eager but vulnerable to overinterpretation.

### Motifs
Lamp flame, table shadows, old voices, doctrine, delay, vigilance.

### Ending Resonance
The church survives by discernment as much as courage.

### Adaptation Cautions
Do not overdo theological exposition. Make doctrinal dispute dramatic through stakes, not lecture.

---

## Chapter 6 — Lost Coordinates

Source title: 잃어버린 좌표

### Narrative Function
Andronicus and Hesthenes approach the hidden Christian gathering. Marriage plans, Christian secrecy, and political risk converge.

### Prose Direction
Wooded, secretive, tactically intelligent. The scene should feel like movement through physical terrain and moral uncertainty at the same time.

### Emotional Temperature
Cautious, strategic, intimate, uneasy.

### Thematic Pressure
Visible public life begins pressing against hidden faith.

### Character Focus
- Andronicus: negotiating love, honor, and imperial responsibility.
- Hesthenes: practical strategist, socially shrewd.
- Hidden church: vulnerable but not passive.

### Motifs
Old ruins, mountain paths, hidden gathering, wedding, promise, secrecy.

### Ending Resonance
The path to love now passes through hidden allegiance.

### Adaptation Cautions
Keep Hesthenes clever without making him comic relief. The tactical wedding plan must carry emotional and political risk.

## Chapter 7 — The Legend of the Shell

Source title: 조개껍질의 전설

### Narrative Function
Wedding celebration, church memory, John’s shell motif, and long personal history begin to merge. Joy is present but shadowed by absence and unresolved pain.

### Prose Direction
Festive surface, sorrowful undertow. Let celebration and memory coexist.

### Emotional Temperature
Warm, nostalgic, unsettled.

### Thematic Pressure
Joy in the present cannot erase wounds carried from the past.

### Character Focus
- John: memory-bearing apostle, still wounded by old history.
- Polycarp and Prochorus: concerned about Callimachus.
- Gaius and orphans: joy mixed with vulnerability.
- Callimachus: absence as pressure.

### Motifs
Shell, wedding feast, children, memory, absent friend, old wound.

### Ending Resonance
A joyful room can still be haunted.

### Adaptation Cautions
Do not allow John’s past to become pure exposition. His memory must feel involuntary and emotionally triggered.

---

## Chapter 8 — The Last Cross

Source title: 마지막 십자가

### Narrative Function
Journey to Rome with John as prisoner. The Appian Way becomes a road of empire, martyrdom, memory, and surrender.

### Prose Direction
Road narrative with heavy historical resonance. The road itself should feel like a witness.

### Emotional Temperature
Solemn, processional, burdened.

### Thematic Pressure
Rome’s road of triumph is also the road of crucified witnesses.

### Character Focus
- John: willing prisoner, carrying memories of Peter, Paul, Mary, Rome, and Vespasian.
- Andronicus: official escort drawn into spiritual history.
- Prochorus and Tychicus: anxious guardians.

### Motifs
Road, chains, cross, triumph, prisoner, dust, old memories.

### Ending Resonance
Empire builds roads for conquest; truth walks them toward witness.

### Adaptation Cautions
Avoid turning the Appian Way into a history essay. Let historical memory surface through John’s inner movement.

---

## Chapter 9 — The Emperor and the Fisherman

Source title: 황제와 어부

### Narrative Function
Domitian and John stand as opposing sovereignties: paranoid imperial power versus aged apostolic witness.

### Prose Direction
Imperial interior vs spiritual stillness. Rome should feel polished, fearful, and claustrophobic.

### Emotional Temperature
Tense, controlled, ominous.

### Thematic Pressure
Power fears what truth does not fear.

### Character Focus
- Domitian: paranoid, intelligent, theatrical, spiritually hollow.
- Domitilla: observant, anxious, politically aware.
- John: not defiant theatrically, but impossible to possess.
- Informers: atmosphere of suspicion.

### Motifs
Dagger, sweat, mirror, palace, informer, interrogation, oil.

### Ending Resonance
The emperor commands bodies; he cannot command witness.

### Adaptation Cautions
Domitian must not become cartoonish. His fear should be psychologically credible.

---

## Chapter 10 — The Quarry Fresco

Source title: 채석장의 벽화

### Narrative Function
Patmos quarry. John’s suffering, endurance, and Revelation atmosphere intensify. Prochorus records and witnesses.

### Prose Direction
Stone, heat, labor, silence. Physical exhaustion must carry spiritual weight.

### Emotional Temperature
Harsh, stripped, prayerful.

### Thematic Pressure
The witness remains when the body is nearly spent.

### Character Focus
- John: old body, unbroken obedience, longing for release.
- Prochorus: loyal attendant and recorder.
- Maximinus: Roman roughness with possible human complexity.

### Motifs
Stone, hammer, dust, sun, wall, vision, wound, endurance.

### Ending Resonance
In the place meant to erase men, revelation begins to mark stone.

### Adaptation Cautions
Do not overdescribe suffering. Let repetitive labor and sparse prayer carry the weight.

# Volume II — Lower Volume

## Chapter 1 — The Banner of Rebellion

Source title: 반역의 깃발

### Narrative Function
Callimachus has become a rebel/bandit leader. Polycarp confronts him. Drusiana’s return triggers pursuit and danger.

### Prose Direction
Rough, tense, morally fractured. Smyrna’s decadence contrasts with Callimachus’s wounded rage.

### Emotional Temperature
Bitter, volatile, urgent.

### Thematic Pressure
A wounded longing for justice can become another empire of the self.

### Character Focus
- Callimachus: charismatic, enraged, ideologically wounded.
- Polycarp: loyal, disturbed, tempted by anger.
- Drusiana: absent presence, moral center under threat.

### Motifs
Wine, theater, horse, flight, anger, stolen road.

### Ending Resonance
Polycarp rides not only to save Drusiana but to keep his own soul from following Callimachus into rage.

### Adaptation Cautions
Moderate explicit exploitation and brutality. Preserve moral danger without sensationalism.

---

## Chapter 2 — The Voice That Returned

Source title: 찾아온 목소리

### Narrative Function
Dawn harbor, John’s return, Revelation interpretation, illness, Nerva’s political shift, and early church eschatological anxiety.

### Prose Direction
Cold dawn, theological uncertainty, restrained expectation.

### Emotional Temperature
Hopeful, anxious, interpretive.

### Thematic Pressure
The church waits for the end while history keeps extending.

### Character Focus
- Tychicus: cautious interpreter, aged realism.
- Polycarp: earnest, eschatologically anxious.
- John: returning presence, not yet fully centered.

### Motifs
Dawn, harbor wind, small boat, plague, prophecy, delay.

### Ending Resonance
The expected ending has not come; faith must learn endurance inside delay.

### Adaptation Cautions
Avoid end-times sensationalism. Keep Revelation discussion humble and historically embedded.

---

## Chapter 3 — The Garden of Heartbreak

Source title: 상심의 화원

### Narrative Function
Drusiana inside Korama underground city. Callimachus’s alternative kingdom is revealed: organized, wounded, vengeful, seductive.

### Prose Direction
Subterranean, ordered, darkly beautiful. A wounded anti-empire that risks becoming empire-like itself.

### Emotional Temperature
Confined, fascinated, sorrowful.

### Thematic Pressure
Those rejected by empire can build a refuge, but revenge can corrupt refuge into another kingdom of force.

### Character Focus
- Drusiana: compassionate, captive, spiritually steady.
- Callimachus: brilliant organizer, wounded savior figure.
- Gaius: vulnerable intelligence shaped by environment.

### Motifs
Stone doors, underground corridors, lamps, hospital, wounds, black garden.

### Ending Resonance
A hidden city can shelter the wounded and still be ruled by vengeance.

### Adaptation Cautions
Do not romanticize coercion. Preserve Drusiana’s moral clarity and captivity reality.

---

## Chapter 4 — Beyond the Years

Source title: 세월을 넘어서

### Narrative Function
John’s public ministry in Ephesus theater. The poor, sick, and forgotten gather. Logos teaching connects Greek thought and gospel.

### Prose Direction
Public yet intimate. John’s voice should move like water through a vast theater.

### Emotional Temperature
Tender, expansive, luminous.

### Thematic Pressure
The Word reaches those empire has made invisible.

### Character Focus
- John: aged preacher, not performer; witness of love and Logos.
- Crowd: poor, sick, curious, skeptical, hungry for love.
- Ephesus citizens: watching Artemis lose emotional monopoly.

### Motifs
Theater, bread, healing, Logos, light, crowd, love.

### Ending Resonance
A city built for spectacle begins hearing a word that spectacle cannot provide.

### Adaptation Cautions
John’s Logos teaching must be readable and powerful without becoming a theological lecture dump.

---

## Chapter 5 — When Losing and Repaying

Source title: 잃을 때와 갚을 때

### Narrative Function
Andronicus after Drusiana’s loss. Grief becomes rage, humiliation, political ambition, and desire for vengeance.

### Prose Direction
Claustrophobic interior madness. Keep psychological realism, reduce lurid sexualized imagination.

### Emotional Temperature
Obsessive, wounded, bitter, unstable.

### Thematic Pressure
Possessive love curdles into vengeance when identity is built on honor.

### Character Focus
- Andronicus: humiliated Roman elite, grieving husband, dangerous strategist.
- Lucius: cautious household stabilizer.
- Nerva/imperial politics: background pressure.

### Motifs
Closed room, wall, summons, horse, palace, sleeplessness, humiliation.

### Ending Resonance
A man who lost love begins seeking restoration through power.

### Adaptation Cautions
Modernize and restrain degrading sexual suspicion. Preserve torment without exploitative imagery.

## Chapter 6 — Horizon of Contest

Source title: 승부의 지평선

### Narrative Function
Callimachus prepares military strategy in Korama. Roman tactics and bandit mobility collide.

### Prose Direction
Strategic, kinetic, intelligent. Battle doctrine should be clear but not dry.

### Emotional Temperature
Focused, disciplined, tense.

### Thematic Pressure
The rejected learn the empire’s methods in order to resist it, risking becoming what they oppose.

### Character Focus
- Callimachus: strategist, teacher, revolutionary commander.
- Gaius: brilliant child, instinctive tactical mind.
- Bandit leaders: rough but responsive to purpose.

### Motifs
Dust, cavalry, map, curved formation, valley, signal flags.

### Ending Resonance
A hidden army learns to move like a living thing.

### Adaptation Cautions
Keep military explanation readable. Use action demonstration rather than abstract lecture when possible.

## Chapter 7 — The Cup and the Gate

Source title: 잔과 문

### Narrative Function
Rome sets a trap at Artemis temple. Andronicus weaponizes Drusiana’s foster parents to draw Callimachus out.

### Prose Direction
Large-scale public dread. Tactical and moral pressure must coexist.

### Emotional Temperature
Severe, calculated, morally cold.

### Thematic Pressure
Empire uses order and spectacle to define rebellion and control public meaning.

### Character Focus
- Andronicus: precise, ruthless, convinced of necessity.
- Ovidius: uneasy political governor.
- Vegetius: military pragmatist.
- Callimachus: absent target whose choice defines public narrative.

### Motifs
Artemis temple, crosses, oil, noon, formation, bait, public square.

### Ending Resonance
Rome’s power is not only military; it controls the meaning of courage and cowardice.

### Adaptation Cautions
Keep brutality restrained but morally chilling. Do not over-explain formations unless needed for stakes.

## Chapter 8 — The Gospel of John I

Source title: 요한의 복음서 (1)

### Narrative Function
John confesses his past: jealousy, ambition, Sophia, Giscala John, Annas, zealotry, and the roots of his spiritual struggle.

### Prose Direction
Confessional, grave, vulnerable. The old apostle’s authority increases because he tells the truth about his own darkness.

### Emotional Temperature
Exposed, sorrowful, cleansing.

### Thematic Pressure
A witness of love was once ruled by rivalry and vengeance.

### Character Focus
- John: not sanitized; deeply human, jealous, ambitious, transformed.
- Papias: recorder, stunned by confession.
- Polycarp: protective, reverent, shaken.

### Motifs
Torchlight, earthquake tremor, confession, shell memory, jealousy, names.

### Ending Resonance
The gospel is guarded not by a flawless man, but by one who has been broken by truth.

### Adaptation Cautions
Handle John’s confession boldly but reverently. Avoid undermining apostolic dignity; show dignity through truthfulness.

## Chapter 9 — The Gospel of John II

Source title: 요한의 복음서 (2)

### Narrative Function
John continues recounting Jesus’ ministry, discipleship politics, Peter rivalry, zealot networks, and his own ambition.

### Prose Direction
Memory as moral unraveling. The past should feel alive but judged by an older conscience.

### Emotional Temperature
Painful, honest, searching.

### Thematic Pressure
Following Jesus did not immediately purify ambition; truth works slowly through exposure.

### Character Focus
- John: younger ambition vs older repentance.
- Peter: rival and genuine leader.
- Judas/Zealots: political complexity.
- Jesus: calm center amid competing ambitions.

### Motifs
Net, road, confession, keys, jealousy, fire, following.

### Ending Resonance
The disciples were not heroes first; they were men being remade.

### Adaptation Cautions
Avoid modern cynicism toward biblical figures. Preserve human complexity within reverence.

## Chapter 10 — The Vine and the Fig Tree

Source title: 포도나무와 무화과

### Narrative Function
John recounts the road to Jerusalem, ambition for seats, Bartimaeus, Bethany, Mary’s anointing, Judas’s betrayal movement.

### Prose Direction
Slow approach to Passion. Human ambition and divine surrender move in opposite directions.

### Emotional Temperature
Foreboding, intimate, tragic.

### Thematic Pressure
Those seeking places of glory do not yet understand the cup.

### Character Focus
- John: remembers his ambition with shame.
- Salome, James, Peter, Judas, Mary of Bethany: each reveals response to Jesus.
- Jesus: moving steadily toward sacrifice.

### Motifs
Cup, seat, road, perfume, table, night, betrayal.

### Ending Resonance
The fragrance of devotion rises while betrayal steps into the dark.

### Adaptation Cautions
Keep Passion-adjacent material solemn. Do not overdramatize what already carries sacred weight.

## Chapter 11 — Maranatha

Source title: 마라나타

### Narrative Function
Arrest, denial, trial, crucifixion trajectory, and John’s memory of failure, fear, and witness.

### Prose Direction
Severe restraint. Sacred history should not be overwritten.

### Emotional Temperature
Dark, stripped, devastating, prayerful.

### Thematic Pressure
Human courage fails; the suffering Christ remains faithful.

### Character Focus
- John: witness, afraid, implicated, remembering.
- Peter: collapse and grief.
- Jesus: quiet sovereignty under judgment.
- Religious and Roman authorities: procedural darkness.

### Motifs
Torch, courtyard, rooster, spit, robe, cross, mother, beloved disciple.

### Ending Resonance
Maranatha is not escape from suffering, but hope after the cross.

### Adaptation Cautions
Use biblical restraint. Avoid adding decorative emotion to crucifixion material.

## Chapter 12 — Author’s Afterword

Source title: 작가의 말 / 쓰고 나서

### Narrative Function
Historical-theological reflection after the novel. Constantine, Ephesus, Artemis, Mary, John, Cappadocia, and the continuing struggle of kingdom vs empire.

### Prose Direction
Essay-like but polished. Should read as reflective afterword, not fiction chapter.

### Emotional Temperature
Reflective, expansive, hopeful, sober.

### Thematic Pressure
The conflict between empire and kingdom continues across history.

### Character / Historical Focus
- Constantine
- Ephesus after Christianity
- Artemis temple stones
- Church memory
- Cappadocia as refuge
- modern continuation of witness

### Motifs
Cross, basilica, stone, pilgrimage, world, hope.

### Ending Resonance
As long as the people of the kingdom remain, humanity still has hope.

### Adaptation Cautions
Modernize rhetoric carefully. Retain authorial conviction while reducing overly sweeping claims where necessary for English readership.
