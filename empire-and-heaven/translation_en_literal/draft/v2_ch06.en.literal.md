# Empire and Heaven (II)

## Chapter 6. The Horizon of the Contest

In the valley of Korama, where the chill autumn sunlight lay spread out, countless men and horses were galloping, raising dust. Between the barren hills lined up as though breathing out red light and the white-eroded ravines, the columns of horses wriggled and pulsed like moving serpents.

The grotesquely shaped rock-mountains here and there, each lifting its head, were riddled with gaping holes. Those rock-mountains, whether clustered together with their holes yawning wide or crouching alone in the distance, gave the beholder an intensely desolate feeling.

The mounted ranks were generally dividing into three branches. The central column spread out and seemed to press forward along the valleys between the rock-mountains, then at once gathered again, shrinking inward and slipping back toward the rear; then, with wings opening on both sides as though driving a hunted beast, they raced outward, only to draw together again toward the center. The countless men and horses were moving organically, like a single living creature.

Callimachus, who had been standing on a high hill directing the cavalry drill, gave the men and horses a rest and then summoned the officers.

"The strength of the Roman legion lies in its large weapons, using machines and devices, and in the siege equipment it constructs with immense material resources. But as you all know, the fortress of Korama is made in such a way that it cannot be attacked by Rome's heavy equipment. Moreover, in order to answer the Roman army's large weapons from within, we have already manufactured and installed crossbows, catapults, and heavy engines."

Callimachus had the chief of staff operations spread a map out upon the ground.

"Now the thing we must do is determine how to use the barrage we have prepared in order to annihilate the Roman army."

Callimachus then had another large diagram spread out beside the map. Upon it were drawn innumerable flags marking units and arrows showing the content of operations.

"In field warfare, the Roman army's specialty is attack upon the rear. Two hundred years ago at Cynoscephalae, when the Roman army struggled against the dense formation of the Macedonian army armed with long spears, the Romans divided their forces and attacked the Macedonian rear, thus annihilating the enemy formation that had seemed like an iron wall. That is to say, the Roman army made the utmost use of its superior numbers and of its own mobility, based on the short sword."

Callimachus drew the dagger from his waist and pointed with its tip to the diagram on the ground.

"Since the battle of Cynoscephalae, the Roman army has continued to develop field formations that make use of its mobility. The design marked here appears extremely complicated, but in a word, it is a multiplication of dispersal."

The boy Gaius, who had been listening to Callimachus's explanation, nodded his head and said,

"In the end, then, this formation is one by which the Roman army tries to overwhelm the enemy by having more troops."

Callimachus smiled faintly and stroked the boy's head.

"That is right. The weakness of an army that moves by means of quantitative superiority is that it lacks flexibility."

The officers of the bandits, having listened to the conversation between Callimachus and the boy Gaius, were bewildered and peering intently at the diagram.

"Gentlemen, if you look carefully at this diagram, you will see that the Roman army's operation is consistent in attacking the rear. But when the Roman army attacks the rear, the opponent will of course not remain still; he too will divide his forces and attack the Roman rear. Then the Roman army again divides its force, and when the Romans divide, the opponent divides again... and so, as though laying bricks, the forces of both sides become piled upon one another, and in this kind of battle the side with the larger number of troops gains the advantage. But the weakness of such a strategy of mass is, as the boy Gaius just pointed out, that it has no flexibility. Just as the Macedonian army lost because its long spears and dense formation kept it from responding swiftly, so the Roman formation too has that weakness. Gaius, explain once more the weakness of the Roman army."

The eyes of the officers turned toward Gaius. Gaius grinned and looked at the old fighters gathered around him.

"As you can see, if the Roman army and the opposing army both overlap their rear attacks, the formations of both armies end up taking the shape of a chaotic melee that can no longer be commanded. In such a battle, the side that can shift to organic action wins."

The officers of the bandits nodded their heads. That was precisely the purpose of the mounted drill they were conducting now. In a melee where men fought entangled in complicated confusion, it was natural that victory would belong to the side that kept its wits.

The words of the boy Gaius continued.

"And one more weakness in the Roman army's operation is that, though their formation seems complex, all of it is linear."

And indeed it was so. The Roman formation was grand and oppressive, dividing into many branches and gathering again, yet when examined closely, it was entirely linear. The boy Gaius smiled faintly as he looked straight at Callimachus.

"When such a linear formation encounters a curved formation that coils about it like a serpent, it loses all strength."

The officers could not hide their amazement at the keen judgment of the boy Gaius. Callimachus stroked his head once more and said,

"Gentlemen, the reason Gaius can state it more accurately than we grown men is that his instinctive wisdom has not yet been dulled. As men grow older, they use only the things they themselves have experienced, stubbornly woven together, as the material of judgment, and so they come to possess judgment poorer than that of children. In any case, you will now understand well the purpose of the mounted drill being conducted today. When the enemy moves with an oppressive linear formation, to confuse it swiftly with a curved formation sounds easy enough in words, but because one must possess exactness and precision through repeated high training, it demands effort twice over."

Having explained that far, Callimachus turned his eyes again to the map.

"As you all know, one legion of the Roman army is composed of twelve hundred spearmen, twelve hundred men of the main force, twelve hundred scouts, and six hundred reserves, with three hundred cavalry supporting them...."

Pointing to the figures one by one with the tip of his dagger, Callimachus began explaining in detail how they could annihilate a Roman legion by means of the formation they were now training. Their formation was like a living serpent, coiling around and bewildering the enemy from unexpected directions, while at the same time moving in perfect order according to the signals of the standard-bearer.

One of the men listening to his explanation asked a question.

"Hearing the dominus's explanation, it is a truly marvelous method of war. But... if the dominus plans to oppose the Roman army in field battle when it attacks?"

"Of course, for us the most advantageous and safest thing is to attack the enemy from inside the caves. But two years ago, the enemy tried every method to attack our caves and in the end failed and withdrew. Therefore, if their commander is not a fool, he will certainly use some method to lure us out into the field. If he does not lure us into field battle, his defeat is plain."

"By what method will they lure us out?"

"I cannot yet predict exactly. But if the enemy is one worthy of being opposed, then by some method or other he will surely lure us out. Now then... let us practice the change of formation once more, and then begin the individual combat drill."

The officers returned all at once to their positions, and the bandits who had been resting were already mounted on their horses. At Callimachus's command, the standard-bearers began signaling. Again in the valley of Korama the dazzling changes of formation began to writhe like a sea anemone. Truly it was advance and retreat governed by flawless calculation. Once caught within their formation, even the most enormous beast seemed as though its four limbs would be tightly wound and bound, leaving it no choice but to struggle and collapse.

Suddenly Callimachus shouted to the standard-bearer of his own direct command.

"Standard-bearer, signal them to draw the whips!"

The flags in both the standard-bearer's hands changed to red. As he sent his signals by changing the positions of the two hands, the bandits riding along the formation all at once drew the whips they had slung across their backs. As the long whips, each seeming at least several fathoms in length, unfurled and cut the empty air all at once, a wind-noise as though heaven and earth were collapsing shook the valley. The whips lashed about wildly as the men rode, and because of their sound the horses ran even more savagely.

"Everything is curved."

The boy Gaius said so while looking upon the spectacle.

"That is right. The Romans are fools who know only straight lines, whether in formation or in weapons. The only thing that can break the edge of a straight line is a curve. We too will use curves in formation and in weapons. When the Roman army comes, they will wave only their swords as if bewitched by ghosts, and there in the valley of Korama they will meet death. Standard-bearer, change to annihilation battle."

When the standard-bearer changed the signal, the bandits on horseback all leapt down and drew the swords from their waists. Their swords too were curved sabers of the kind used in the Persian army. Holding the whip in the left hand and swinging it, they slashed like lightning with the saber from within the shadow of the whip. The whole valley was overflowing with blue murderous force.

At some point, in the valley of Korama filled with bloodthirst, the shadows of three men appeared. Their expressions were stiff from the chill wind of September and the hair-raising violence of the valley, yet without hesitation they were walking into the training ground where countless whips and swords were cutting the air.

When they had approached near the training ground, more than ten bandits suddenly sprang out, drew their curved sabers, and surrounded the three men. The sabers caught the autumn sunlight and flashed with a terrible light.

"Who are you fellows?"

One of the bandits asked in a fierce expression. The man standing in the middle of the three answered,

"We have come to meet Callimachus."

Callimachus was looking down upon the scene from above on the hill. Even from the hill he could clearly see the figures of his men encircling the three strangers who had entered from outside. Callimachus gave an order to a bandit of the staff standing beside him.

"Go down and see what it is."

"Yes, dominus."

The man who had received the order mounted his horse and was already racing down in that direction like an arrow. Callimachus looked at the boy Gaius and said,

"Gaius."

"Yes?"

"How is it? If the legionaries of Rome come in force, do you think you too can fight along with us?"

"Of course. The masters who taught me martial skill always praised my ability."

"And yet... you still seem too young to go out to war and die."

"I did not think you would say such a thing. You always said that for a warrior of Korama, the question is not when he dies but how he dies."

"Gaius... do you not want to return to Ephesus?"

The boy Gaius looked at Callimachus's face and grinned.

"I too... I too am an orphan with nowhere to receive me, just like you, Uncle."

In that instant Callimachus's eyes were reddening. Gaius too was a lonely outcast with no place under heaven to belong. Callimachus knew too well the pain of that from his own childhood. And now Gaius too would have to live through such years. He too would have to walk that same road. Perhaps the pity Callimachus felt toward Gaius sprang from just that likeness of kind. One was the son of an apostate, and the other the son of a martyr, but it was all the same that both were orphans.

"Damn it...."

Callimachus kicked a small stone lying before his eyes. Since childhood it had been his habit, whenever his heart was hurt, to vent it upon a stone. Then Gaius too kicked the stone before him. And after that, Gaius stared straight again at Callimachus's face.

"Uncle... you are not regretting it, are you?"

"Regretting what?"

"Because even if you keep fighting like this all the time, it will not bring any real end to anything."

"?"

"Think about it. Two years ago Uncle Andronicus came attacking with a cavalry cohort and went back defeated... and now he is bent on fighting again."

Callimachus said through clenched teeth,

"...If one of the two dies, the fight will end."

Gaius laughed shortly and kicked another stone.

"Adults... I truly cannot understand them."

"What is there that you cannot understand?"

"...What I mean is that I cannot understand why men must fight like that over a single woman. There are plenty of beautiful women in the world, so why must they fight one another over just one woman...?"

In truth, it was a remark that struck Callimachus in a painful place. When all was said and done, Callimachus had become a bandit because Drusiana was taken from him, and the fight with Andronicus had begun because he had taken Drusiana back again. And it was not merely a struggle between two men over one woman, but had changed into a collective conflict in which the lives of many had to be risked.

"Gaius... when you grow a little more, you too will understand. A man comes to risk his honor and his life in order to possess the woman he loves."

Gaius was smiling again.

"I... I will never like women at all. Women are narrow-hearted, fickle, and troublesome, so I hate them. Especially when I see two uncles fighting because of a woman, it makes me think women are not something one ought to draw near to. I would rather live easily alone like the Apostle John."

When the name of the Apostle John came from the mouth of Gaius, Callimachus again made an unpleasant face. He spat upon the ground.

"Why do you make such an unpleasant face whenever one speaks of the Apostle John?"

"That old man is a hypocrite!"

"There, you see."

"Before, how much you used to follow the Apostle John. You followed and loved him almost like your own father, and now you hate him as though he were an enemy... and all because of a woman. It is because the Apostle John performed the wedding of Uncle Andronicus and Sister Drusiana, is it not?"

"It is not because of that! Every word from that old man is rotten through!"

Because Callimachus's face had become fierce, Gaius shrank his neck. The bandit from the staff who had gone down below was riding back up the hill.

"What was it?"

"They say that three men from outside wish to meet the dominus."

"Where do they say they are from?"

"They say they have come from Ephesus."

"From Ephesus? What did they say their names were?"

"One says he is Papias, and the others say Polycarp and Prochorus."

A delighted light came into the eyes of the boy Gaius. But Callimachus said with a hard face,

"There is no need to meet them. Tell them to go back."

"Understood."

The boy Gaius looked at Callimachus and said,

"If they have come from Ephesus... might we not hear information about Uncle Andronicus?"

Then the bandit of the staff, who had been about to turn and go back down, also stopped once more and said,

"That is true. If Trajanus of the military has indeed ascended the throne, there seems likely to be some change in military policy, but accurate information has not yet reached Korama. Yet the three fellows below say they have come to tell the dominus something important."

Callimachus was looking at him with his brow furrowed. They too could not help but want to know about the information upon which their lives depended. Moreover, because the man did not know the relationship between those three men below and Callimachus, he could not understand why his chief would refuse to meet them. Callimachus, still frowning, said to him,

"Very well. Bring them up."

"Yes. I will bring them at once."

The man was again running down the hill. Callimachus turned back to Gaius and said,

"Gaius...."

"Yes, Uncle."

"Do you... do you think Sister Drusiana too is a narrow-hearted, fickle, troublesome woman?"

"Not exactly, but... in any case women are bound to be less than men."

"Then... do you mean you have no thought of marrying and living with a woman? Who will do your washing and who will bear your children?"

"That is exactly it. I will never have children."

"...?"

"I... I hate growing up as an orphan so much that I will never let another child like me be born again. As you yourself say, no one knows where or how a man will die, so why bear children only to make them orphans again? Going from house to house to beg a meal, starving if no one feeds them, sleeping anywhere at all... I will never beget another child like that!"

Again tears filled the eyes of Callimachus. He put an arm around Gaius's shoulder so that the boy could not see his face.

"Gaius... for such a little fellow you worry over all kinds of things. But in any case, a human being is born into this world already cast off. There is no one to care for him and no one to interfere with him. Look at the young of beasts. They are born any old way in the mountains or the fields, yet they live out their lives in their own fashion. Of course some young are cared for by their mothers, but there are also many beasts that must live on their own from the very moment they are born. Is that not magnificent? My life I myself will live. That is the road a man goes."

"Uncle, I... I did not find it magnificent at all. Not for me, and I do not think anyone in this world lives magnificently. I... I think it is wrong to bring children into this world for nothing. If only there were no people in this world... the world would be peaceful."

A lonely wind was blowing through the heart of Callimachus. The childish babbling of this little boy was making his breast ache with chill.

The figures of people climbing the hill came into view. Following the bandit who guided them, familiar faces were approaching. Looking at those faces, Callimachus stood motionless like a stone image. The boy Gaius ran out to meet them. Polycarp among them received the running boy in his arms. The boy's face had been roughened by the northern wind, and a sword and a whip were strapped to his little body, yet still his child-heart, seeking the people he had missed, was burning warm.

"Uncle...."

"Gaius, it has been a long time."

Gaius exchanged greetings also with Papias and with Prochorus. Then they approached Callimachus, who was standing tall upon the hill. Papias went first toward Callimachus with both arms spread.

"Callimachus... I have missed you terribly all this while."

The two men embraced one another and exchanged a calm greeting. Polycarp, because he had already once engaged Callimachus in fierce argument at the Pagus theater of Smyrna, embraced him somewhat awkwardly, while Prochorus said in a strong voice befitting his sturdy frame,

"Callimachus, I had always taken you for a mere scholar, but when I heard you had become a bandit, I was astonished. This seems rather the sort of thing that ought to suit a fellow like me."

Callimachus gave a brief smile and answered Prochorus,

"The bandits of our Korama always keep the gates open. If you wish to enter, we will receive you at any time."

Papias took hold of Callimachus's shoulder and said,

"Callimachus... surely you do not mean to entertain friends who have come so far in this chilly place?"

Callimachus thought a moment and then said to the bandit from the staff,

"Deliver this to the training commander. I am going briefly to headquarters to speak with the guests, so the drills planned for today are to be completed without failure by the appointed hour."

"Yes, I will give the message."

Watching the man turn and run off, Callimachus said to his friends,

"Come... I will show you what a bandits' den looks like. It may not match the great mansion of Andronicus, but there will at least be a place where you may sit and rest for a while...."

Callimachus was still speaking with a barb. Those three had all been men who had received the banquet in Andronicus's great mansion. The boy Gaius went first, and Callimachus and his three friends followed behind him.

They entered a worn-out hut halfway up the mountain. But inside the hut there were only two bandits standing guard, with no sign that anyone lived there. Yet when they entered the second room of the hut, the great stone door that blocked one side of the wall opened with a rumbling sound, and the mouth of a dark passage yawned open.

They entered the mouth of the cave. Lamps were lit here and there along the walls of the cave, yet because of the rays entering from somewhere, it was not very dark. But that was no wonder to them, since they already knew how the cave of Coresus in Ephesus drew in sunlight.

Prochorus ran his hand over the wall of the passage and said,

"This mountain too... is made of volcanic tuff like Mount Coresus in Ephesus and the underground tombs of Rome."

Volcanic tuff not only hardened with many holes already pierced through it, but was also softer in composition than other rock and therefore good for excavation.

As they followed the boy Gaius farther and farther inward, they were realizing that this underground city of Korama was of a scale utterly incomparable with either the cave of Mount Coresus or the catacombs of Rome. The structure inside the cave was highly precise, and stone doors capable of being mechanically moved had been installed wherever one went. Papias asked,

"In all... how many people can this underground city hold?"

"There are already more than ten thousand people in this cave alone."

"What? Then do you mean there is yet another underground city besides this one?"

"Besides this one there is another underground city, and each city is again linked to the others by underground passages."

The three friends were giving cries of amazement. Polycarp said,

"If the scale is this enormous... then it truly would be a place fit to take refuge in during the time of the final tribulation of which the Apostle wrote in Revelation."

"Revelation...."

"When the Apostle was arrested and serving in the quarry on the island of Patmos, he saw visions concerning the end of the world, and Prochorus wrote them down. According to that record, at the end of mankind a false Christ will appear and rule the world, and he will stamp a number upon the forehead of every person, and everyone without a number will be put to death."

"That sounds like a tale thought up after seeing the information politics of Domitian."

Callimachus spoke as though he believed in no such revelation.

"Still... the idea of putting a number on the forehead is truly ingenious."

"Then would it not be enough simply to receive the number?"

"The false Christ who appears anew... will make all men use that number whenever they buy or sell or travel. If that happens, every movement of the people will be recorded in the ruler's ledger one by one, and man will become a being without freedom, like a slave."

Callimachus said in a cold tone,

"That old man John...."

Papias and Prochorus, since they had already heard from Polycarp about Callimachus's altered way of speaking, were not surprised that he spoke so.

"He was probably wearing a prison number when he was in the quarry, and so he could think of such a thing."

"Perhaps... perhaps such an experience did play upon it. Yet what that numbering signifies is clearly a warning against the systems of this world. No matter how excellent a method of rule may be, a method of rule without freedom makes man a slave. Is not that precisely the falsity of the kingdoms of this world, and what we learned as the kingdom of God not an alternative to that falsity? Is there not perhaps a kingdom where one is not bound by institutions, not toyed with by falsehood, not swept away by rage...? Was not that the very truth we were seeking? Ah... this was exactly what you always used to say, Callimachus."

Callimachus answered still in a chilly voice,

"There is no such dream-like kingdom in this world. I have already decided to wake from that dream. The way I live is my kingdom. I have resolved to be faithful to myself and true to myself. That alone is the way to live without being deceived by malicious trickery. There is nothing more true than oneself. Every truth takes oneself as its standard. I have no one to trust but myself. No one is me."

They kept on speaking of such things as they climbed and descended stairs several times and walked through passageways. The friends who had come from Ephesus had arrived with prior knowledge, and so avoided directly colliding with Callimachus's words. At last they passed the stone door of headquarters, where two guards stood watch, and came to the front of the chief's office. Again the stone door opened, and they entered a wide room kept clean and orderly.

Prochorus looked around the room and said,

"Ho... what a splendid room. Callimachus, except for the name bandit, it seems you have succeeded rather well. At this rate you might well be called the emperor of the underworld."

At Prochorus's absurd remark, Callimachus again gave a brief smile. Offering seats to his friends, he said,

"For you Christiani... it means there is now one more strong enemy. The Roman Empire was already a strong enemy to you, and now again Callimachus of Korama has appeared as a new enemy."

By calling himself the enemy of all Christiani, Callimachus was trying to overawe the three friends. Papias drew himself up and said,

"Callimachus, since you know that the teaching of Jesus Christ is not struggle but reconciliation, why do you speak that way? Jesus Christ came to save all mankind, not to judge them. Our enemy is neither Rome nor Korama, but only the power behind that tempts man... that is, the authority of Satan. And that authority of Satan, that is, the authority of this world, is not working only in the Roman Empire. Wherever struggle breaks out, there Satan works in secret. In Rome, in Korama, and even among the Christiani, wherever man hates man, wherever man tries to rule man, wherever man refuses to acknowledge the authority of God, there the authority of Satan is."

Again Callimachus shot a cold smile and glared at Papias.

"Who made that monster you call Satan? To lay all faults upon Satan is nothing but cowardice, shifting one's own responsibility onto another. Why can you not speak frankly? Why can you not say, My enemy is the emperor of Rome, my enemy is Callimachus of Korama?"

Again Callimachus was subtly boasting of his own power by placing himself alongside the emperor of Rome.

"Callimachus...."

This time Polycarp stepped forward. Though he knew it was not good to be drawn further and further into argument, he could not pass over such matters in silence.

"Callimachus, because all people are children of God, no one is the enemy of the Christiani. We merely labor that they may escape from the deceit of the world and return, and you yourself too were once serving for that very end. Because of your departure there has come to be a great emptiness in the kingdom of God. We need you. You must return...."

Callimachus laughed loudly toward the empty air.

"So it would be... If the God you believe in cannot even keep one of His own children and so makes Him grieve, how can He guard His kingdom? Your God will go on disappointing His children, and they will flee away, and that kingdom will become full of holes and collapse."

"Callimachus...."

Papias, not retreating, opened his mouth.

"Rather the situation is advancing in the opposite direction. The power of Satan is collapsing, and all who had been bound are returning. One day even the emperor of Rome will cast off the chains of Satan and return to God. That was your faith, and it is our faith."

Prochorus, who had been silent, leaned his upper body forward and broke in.

"You have heard how the kingdom of God is expanding. You know of the martyrdom of Consul Clemens, do you not? He was the administrative head of Rome, the ruler next only to the emperor. And not only that, his wife Domitilla was the emperor's own niece, and yet she willingly presented herself and embraced the glory of martyrdom. Now Rome itself is almost inside the kingdom of God as far as the nape of its neck."

Callimachus still did not change his chilly expression.

"You people have become utterly one party with Rome. In that case, it would be best for you to go and negotiate with them. Go to Rome and acknowledge their gods and ask them kindly to make room among those gods for your god Jehovah or for Jesus. If you do that, a temple of Jehovah or a shrine of Jesus will be built in the city of Rome... and you people, invited to the houses of the rich, will eat rich foods and pray for their wealth and glory... that would truly be a case of suiting both sister and brother-in-law."

The venom of Callimachus's words was fiercely scratching the nerves of the three friends. They still remembered the rich feast at the wedding of Andronicus. Yet Papias, striving not to give way to rage, said,

"Callimachus... God is on no one's side. Partiality is not justice. If, because you hate Rome, you insist that justice belongs only to Korama, is that not also partiality?"

"So it seems that though Jesus was once the friend of the poor, your doctrine has changed these days."

"As you know, Jesus said that it is hard for the rich to enter heaven. That we all acknowledge. Yet if a rich man too abandons the greed of this world and returns, no one can stop him. That is why we believed Him not as the Messiah of the Jews, but as the hope of all mankind."

"So then Jesus of Galilee has at last begun wishing to attend the parties held in the palaces of Rome?"

"Not attendance, but salvation. That is why the Lord Jesus did not join in the rage of the people of Jerusalem who wished to take revenge upon Rome, and Judas Iscariot, displeased with that attitude, tried to make the Savior of comfort and peace into an incarnation of rage and revenge. Thus in the end he became the man who betrayed the Savior."

Then Callimachus suddenly began to laugh with a snickering sound.

"Exactly. Now I remember. I honor that Judas Iscariot. How his heart must have burned with frustration, following that stifling Jesus. All the teaching of Jesus is nothing but excuses for defeat. Because men resist with such ideas, they lose countless lives to the power of Rome and still go on living in the light of Rome. Yes. I like Judas Iscariot. I truly find him to my taste. He was a splendid man. He grew angry at Jesus' weakness, and so he threw away that false, sham-like hypocrisy. And because it pained him as a man to have betrayed his teacher, he committed suicide like a man. What a man does, that man himself must answer for. How much more manly he was than the other disciples who ran away? I hope you too will become such men...."

"Men who commit suicide?"

"I... I have no need to commit suicide. Jesus was never my teacher!"

At that moment, from the direction of the door, women in highly provocative clothing came in and set teacups and fruits upon the table. Their clothes were so thin that the flesh beneath was plainly visible, and from their bodies came a strange scent that shook the human heart.

"Come now... it may not match the delicacies of the house of Andronicus, but these are famous products of Cappadocia, so taste them. And if you wish... you may stay for several days. I will assign each of you one of the young women who just came in."

Callimachus was speaking a thoroughly absurd thing. Papias was bishop of the church in Hierapolis, and Polycarp too was bishop of the church in Smyrna. Callimachus was now proposing to those men, the highest leaders of the churches, that he would assign a woman to each of them.

Callimachus lifted the teacup before him and drained it in gulps, then looked around at the three friends before his eyes.

"According to my subordinate's report... you have not come to tell me something important, have you? Surely you did not come all the way here to preach at me?"

When Callimachus changed the subject, Papias seemed to know that the time had come to speak the main matter, and he looked somewhat tense. He lifted the teacup before him, drank one sip, and opened his mouth.

"...The reason we have come in this way today is to inform you of one thing."

He drank tea once more and then said in a clear voice,

"Governor Lycomedes of Ephesus petitioned to resign...."

"Governor Lycomedes?"

"Yes. He received baptism from the Apostle John and became one of the Christiani, and when that began to be rumored, he petitioned to resign beforehand."

Callimachus looked puzzled for a moment. Governor Lycomedes was the very man who had persecuted many Christiani and had them executed in the arena. Papias watched the face of Callimachus a moment as he sat thus, then continued.

"The emperor accepted Governor Lycomedes's resignation and appointed Marcus Ovidius, the jurist of the Senate, as governor of Ephesus."

"Ovidius? Is he not the head of the progressive faction?"

"That is right. But Emperor Trajanus appointed Marius Andronicus under that governor as curator."

"And what matters even more... according to the emperor's announcement, he has given the curator under the governor of Ephesus the authority to request the mobilization of the forces stationed in Asia Minor. Therefore, when Andronicus takes up his post in Ephesus, he will certainly dispatch legionary forces in order to attack Korama."

"I had already foreseen that. That is why the cavalry of Korama are training to crush the Roman legion. But... are you telling me that you came all the way to this far place, three of you together, merely to tell me that information?"

Papias hesitated slightly, yet at once continued his speech again.

"Callimachus... we have come to ask one thing of you. If the great force of Korama and the Roman legions collide, there will be immense slaughter, and so we ask whether the collision might not be avoided."

Callimachus opened his eyes wide and glared at Papias.

"So after all you have come, standing on the side of Andronicus, to persuade me?"

Callimachus struck the table with his fist and sprang to his feet.

"Who sent you? Was it Andronicus, or John who likes to eat at his expense? Since when have you Christiani sunk into becoming brokers who wander about mediating wars?"

"Callimachus... no one sent us. And to speak frankly, what we are anxious over is not the bandits of Korama, nor the legions of Rome either."

"...Promotion comes fast."

"Then, then why do you come to me saying such things?"

"It is because... it is because we are concerned for you, Callimachus."

"What?"

"Callimachus, we are still your unchanging friends. If the fact that we once went to Andronicus's house and ate a supper he gave us is so bitter to you, then I apologize to you. But at least we... believe that the friendship between you and us was not so fragile that it could be cracked by one woman. Of course we are grieved that you failed to win Drusiana's choice. Yet in any case, Drusiana chose that man, and he received baptism from the Apostle, and said he wished his marriage to be solemnized by him. In that moment, how should the Apostle have answered? Should he have said that he could not perform the ceremony because Drusiana was fit to be the bride of Callimachus, whom he regarded like a true son? And what should we have done? To a believer newly entered into the faith, should we have said, because we are friends of Callimachus, we cannot attend the wedding feast? To speak frankly, Callimachus...."

Papias had spoken too quickly, and paused a moment to recover his breath.

"...Callimachus, to speak frankly, at least to the three of us, you are more important than either Andronicus or Drusiana. Even if you resist and stand against the Lord's teaching, that is something the Lord Himself can one day call you back from, but we simply want to keep you from rushing into danger over so trivial a matter. At the very least, we do not want you to be placed in danger. If you die, even our hope that the Lord will call you back again will collapse."

"Papias...."

The enraged voice of Callimachus was slowly sinking.

"Papias... this is not a simple emotional matter. It is a war of revenge being waged by Marius Andronicus, who in the confidence of his own brilliance looks down on Korama and wants to display his power. He is an arrogant fellow representing the insolence of the Roman Empire. That is why the warriors of our Korama fight. Until Rome realizes for itself that it has no power to rule the whole world, and until Rome perceives that its dream was vain, we will trample Rome down and crush it. That is the revenge of the people of Korama, who have lived under contempt."

"Callimachus... do you think you can subdue Rome by force? Rome is not an empire moved by one or two privileged men. What rules Rome is the anxiety and restlessness that spring from human sin and endless greed, the psychological shrinking and fear that if a man lives honestly he may fall behind, and the herd impulse by which because of that men keep binding themselves into ever greater organizations. Callimachus, how do you mean to stand and fight against the twenty-eight legions of this tower of Babel?"

"That is precisely why you Christiani are cowards. If you are frightened because Rome possesses twenty-eight legions, you may go on being crushed under Rome forever and never know when it will end. If the peoples of the whole world rose up, what is there to fear in Rome's legions? I mean to rise in revolt precisely to awaken those sleeping peoples. At the very least, men must be awakened to the fact that a man ought to die fighting. To die upon a sickbed like an old dog is the shame of a great man."

"Callimachus... even if you overthrow the Roman Empire, the worldly power that rules mankind will raise its head again. The history of the Jewish people proves it. When the Jews fell into idol worship, Egypt came upon them, and Babylon carried them away, and Media and Persia ruled them again. When those powers withdrew, then Alexander's Macedonia conquered them, and when Alexander died, Rome in turn ruled them. What is idolatry? It is precisely when man, seized by his own greed, abandons God and runs after the false image he himself has made. As long as man is apart from God, the spiderweb that binds mankind will tighten day by day. Even if Rome vanishes, a stronger Rome will appear in this world. If you, Callimachus, should conquer Rome, you will only build another Rome."

Callimachus again laughed with a snickering sound.

"Exactly. That is the flow of history. The world is ruled by the strong and by the victorious. If I crush Rome, I will become a stronger ruler still. Cowards will be sent to the labor gangs, and only the courageous will stand in the place of rule. That is why I fight Rome with a sense of mission. I will set the warriors of Korama free from the resentment of having been unjustly driven here."

The air in the room was growing heavy. Polycarp and Prochorus both kept silent, and even Papias seemed to be growing weary of trying to persuade this chief of bandits. As though unable to bear the suffocation, the boy Gaius broke in.

"Bishop Papias... if one wished to avoid collision with the Roman legions, what method would there be?"

Papias stroked Gaius's head and said,

"If one is to hold peace talks... then one must negotiate on the basis of the matter that became the cause of the war."

"Then... if Sister Drusiana were returned, might peace talks perhaps be established?"

"...That too could be one possible method."

Gaius looked mischievously at Callimachus.

"What do you think, Uncle Callimachus? What if you sent Sister Drusiana back again to Ephesus? If, as you say, the war between Korama and Rome is not a simple emotional matter... then should not a woman be something so trivial that she may be sent back?"

Yet in truth, these days Callimachus had been seriously considering sending Drusiana back to Ephesus. It was something he had begun to think about last spring, when Drusiana had been bitten by the viper and had hovered at the edge of death. Perhaps it had indeed been too much to drag her away using the struggle between men as an excuse.

It had been partly because he was so furious at Drusiana, who had abandoned him and decided to marry Andronicus. Yet when he saw that Drusiana, once brought captive, cared for the patients of Korama without a single complaint and even gave thought to him himself, he had come back again to the position of the one who had protected her when they were children.

From that point, in fact, Callimachus no longer wished to keep Drusiana detained within these caves of Korama. He felt that one ought not to shut a pitiable bird inside a cage.

Yet even when he tried to send her back, there was one reason he could not bring himself to do it. Not only the patients of Korama, but all the inhabitants too followed her too dearly. They served her almost as the mistress of this underground city, and within their alienated lives the warm presence of Drusiana had become a comfort that could not be ignored.

"Gaius...."

Callimachus looked into the bright eyes of Gaius.

"Do you too... do you too want to return to Ephesus?"

Papias, Polycarp, and Prochorus all grew tense at once. The words Callimachus was asking Gaius clearly had as their premise the return of Drusiana.

Gaius shook his head.

"I... I will stay with Uncle Callimachus."

"Do you mean... that you would be all right even apart from Sister Drusiana?"

A lonely light passed for an instant through the eyes of Gaius. Drusiana, who from the time he had still been a sniveling child had fed him at the academy of Tyrannus; Drusiana, who when she went to Rome as a new bride had taken only him along out of all the orphans; Drusiana, who despite that lonely life in Rome had lived relying only upon him. And yet now the boy Gaius too had reached the point where he must emerge from the bosom of a woman and enter upon his own lonely road. And Drusiana was important to him, but somehow he also felt that he could not part from Callimachus.

Just then. From the direction of the door, a bandit suddenly came in and said in a breathless voice,

"Dominus, a communication has come from Marius Andronicus under the governor of Ephesus."

"Wha...?"

Everyone in the room turned in surprise toward the one who had entered. He held out a sealed letter to Callimachus. Callimachus received it and tore open the envelope.

"Let this be known to Callimachus. On September 25 at noon, I desire to fight a decisive battle with your bandit horde upon the field before the temple of Artemis in Ephesus. I trust you will answer this challenge like a man, but fearing lest you should not answer, I have decided to take Drusianus and his wife of the house of Tyrannus as hostages. If you and your horde do not appear by noon on that day, Drusianus and his wife will be suitably roasted upon two crosses set up before the temple.... Curator Marius Andronicus."

Drusianus and his wife were none other than the parents of Drusiana, the benefactors who had raised Callimachus, and to Andronicus the father-in-law and mother-in-law. Crumpling the letter in his hand, Callimachus cried out,

"Cowardly wretch, I knew you would do this. I will teach you the taste of the sword of Korama!"
