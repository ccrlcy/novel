# Empire and Heaven (II)

## Chapter 11. Maranatha

The place where the soldiers and servants who arrested Jesus took him, bound, was the house of Annas. Already in Annas's house torches were lit here and there, and at every entrance security was strict. John, who often came and went from this house and had become familiar with the servants there, entered through the gate without any restraint and walked inside, but then suddenly realized that Peter was not following him and turned back. Peter was at the gate, struggling while being held back by the servants.

John returned again toward the gate, saw the maidservant there who knew him, and asked her to let Peter in. Then that maidservant looked straight at Peter and suddenly pointed at him and said,

"Hey there, you are the disciple who used to follow the Nazarene, aren't you?"

Peter, who was already tense, jumped and said,

"A... no."

Peter was barely able to get past their obstruction and enter inside. John told him to stand among the people warming themselves by the fire and watch closely, and then he approached the inner courtyard and observed what was happening to Jesus. Annas was already interrogating Jesus in the courtyard.

"What did you teach the people?"

"...I spoke openly to the people. I did nothing in secret. Ask those who heard me."

The words of the Teacher struck John's heart like a dagger while he listened from among the people. John himself was among those who had heard Jesus. Yet now he was not only failing to testify to what he had heard from his Teacher, he was hiding and watching him instead. At that moment one of Annas's servants struck Jesus on the cheek and rebuked him.

"Your manner of speaking to a high official is far too insolent!"

Annas looked down at Jesus and, as though he no longer found him worth dealing with, handed the interrogation of Jesus over to his son-in-law Caiaphas and withdrew.

The woman whom Peter had seen earlier by the gate came up to him again and pointed at him at that very moment while he was warming himself by the fire and watching the inner courtyard.

"You.... there's no doubt. I saw you with the Nazarene!"

But Peter again pretended not to know and shook his head.

"I do not know what this woman is talking about."

Just as he was saying that and slowly trying to leave the place, even the other people standing nearby looked at Peter's face and began speaking one by one.

"From the way you speak, you are a Galilean. Didn't Jesus of Nazareth plan to establish his kingdom by taking only Galileans?"

Peter spat on the ground in disbelief and muttered,

"How filthy this is! How could I know such a man? I know nothing of such a fellow!"

At that moment the sound of a rooster crowing came from afar. Only then did Peter hear Jesus' words from the upper room at Mark's house together with the crowing of the rooster.

- Before the rooster crows tonight, you will deny me three times....

As if Jesus' eyes, which had looked at him while saying that, were still piercing him now, Peter tore at his own chest. The three years since he had first met Jesus, when he had become needlessly excited and followed him wildly everywhere, flashed through his mind like a revolving lantern. Peter withdrew from that place and, wiping the rising tears away with a large fist, walked into the dark night like a child crying out loud.

What the high priest Caiaphas had to do first after receiving the interrogation of Jesus from his father-in-law Annas was secure evidence. Evidence was needed in order to accuse Jesus. For the gathering of evidence he called in the people who would denounce Jesus. Several men stepped forward and testified that Jesus had insulted the temple.

"This man said that if the temple were torn down, he could build it in three days."

But their testimonies did not agree with each other. Another person's testimony was a little different from that.

"What I heard was a little different.... What I heard was.... that he would tear down the temple made by hands and build another temple not made by hands in three days."

Jewish law required the testimony of two or more people to match in order for it to be valid. Since dawn was approaching and Jesus had to be handed over to the governor by then, Caiaphas grew anxious as time kept dragging on. He asked Jesus directly.

"Did you yourself say that you are the Son of God, the Messiah?"

Then Jesus, who had been silent until now, slowly lifted his head and spoke.

"If you are a person who knows God.... you will see the Son of Man coming on the clouds of heaven."

As he said that, Jesus looked at Caiaphas's face with a calm expression. That was the proclamation of the Messiah's coming. But Caiaphas, who had already violated their own rules by convening the council in the middle of the night, could not face the brightness of the light coming upon the clouds out of the darkness. He tore his own garments with his own hands and cried out,

"What further testimony do we need? What do you all think?"

When Caiaphas's condemnation of Jesus became clear, people began to assault Jesus. They struck him with their fists, slapped him with their hands, spat on him, and mocked him with insults. At last the Sanhedrin decided to hand Jesus over to the governor, and as soon as day broke they escorted him away.

Originally Pilate had no intention of getting involved in the matter of Jesus. He only needed to focus on maintaining public order as instructed by the Roman emperor, and from his point of view he could foresee that if he got entangled in what seemed like a provincial religious dispute, his own position would become troublesome. Originally the Roman Empire had made religious freedom a principle, so the governor had no need to interfere in their religion.

Yet the Sanhedrin was accusing Jesus of treason.

"What treason has this man committed?"

"This fellow incited the Jews not to pay taxes."

Pilate gave a cold laugh when he heard the priests.

"As for taxes, I have already received a report. This man did not say not to pay taxes to Caesar; he said, 'Render to Caesar the things of Caesar, and to God the things of God.'"

Indeed, the governor Pilate's information was accurate. But the priests did not back down and held firm.

"Your Excellency, that is only part of the evidence of this fellow's treason. This man planned to stir up rebellion by inciting the Galileans."

"Galilee...? Is this man from Galilee? Then that is the jurisdiction of King Herod."

At that time Herod happened to be in Jerusalem. Pilate quickly transferred Jesus' case to Herod. But the foxlike Herod was not going to fall for Pilate's scheme. Getting involved in Jesus' case was equally useless and harmful for him. He toyed with Jesus' face, which he had only heard about in rumors, and said to the priests,

"This fellow is not some tremendous magician as the rumors said, but an ordinary young man.

I have not yet received any report that this young man committed acts of rebellion in the region of Galilee. If this man caused trouble in Jerusalem, then he is naturally under the jurisdiction of Governor Pilate."

In the end, Jesus' case returned again to Pilate. When Pilate frowned and showed his irritation, the Sanhedrin at last began to threaten him.

"If you do not deal with this clear case of treason, Your Excellency too will become a traitor to Caesar."

Unable to withstand the Sanhedrin's persistent threats, Pilate reluctantly began to interrogate Jesus.

"Are you the king of the Jews?"

"What you say is correct. But my kingdom does not belong to this world."

"What sort of kingdom is that?"

"It is the kingdom of heaven where truth is alive. I have come to testify to that."

"What is truth?"

But Jesus remained silent. Truth was standing right in front of Governor Pilate. Pilate somehow felt fear from this man. He was unlike the people Pilate had dealt with until now. Whenever everyone else stood before Pilate, they trembled in fear, or else resisted or cursed him, but this man was not like that. Rather, he was looking at the governor with a sympathetic expression, as though looking at a pitiful person.

At that moment the chief steward came up to Pilate and delivered a note sent by his wife. Pilate barely escaped Jesus' gaze and unfolded the note.

- Please do not get entangled in the case of the Nazarene. I had a very bad dream last night because of that man.

Pilate frowned again. A broad forehead was a characteristic of Roman men. He said to Jesus in an irritated voice,

"Hey, these many people are accusing you, so why do you not defend yourself?"

But Jesus remained silent. Truth could not defend itself. One idea came into Pilate's mind. It had been the custom of the Jewish governor at every Passover, the festival of the Jews, to grant one prisoner whom they wanted a special pardon. It was a favor of the Roman Empire in order to appease the people of the province. He brought Jesus before the Jews and said,

"Behold this man. I have found no fault in him. I intend to release this man as the Passover amnesty."

But a strange reaction began to appear among the Jewish crowd. The name they were waving their fists and demanding be released was unexpectedly Barabbas, the Zealot leader who had recently been arrested after causing a disturbance. Pilate could not at all understand the Jews' demand and intent. From Pilate's point of view, the Zealots were also a resistance force centered on Galilee, and the Jesus they had accused was likewise suspected of plotting rebellion with the Galileans. So if they were going to ask for the release of a rebel, they should naturally ask for Jesus, the chief figure, yet they were asking for Barabbas, a mere minor official of the Zealots. This made it clear that the charge of rebellion had been forcibly pinned on Jesus.

"Then what do you want me to do with this Jesus?"

"He must be crucified!"

"Crucify him!"

It was truly unexpected. The Jews had denounced as a man who had plotted for Jewish independence and demanded that he be subjected to crucifixion, a punishment normally applied to notorious traitors.

Yet the noise of the crowd was growing louder and louder. There was a feeling that something was seriously going wrong. The advisers standing nearby warned Pilate.

"If this goes on, a riot is likely to break out."

"It would be better to grant the people's request. If a riot breaks out, Your Excellency will only be reprimanded by His Majesty the Emperor for nothing."

In the end Pilate regarded the noisy crowd as more dangerous than Jesus, and finally had no choice but to hand Jesus over to them. He looked at Jesus with a pitiful expression and said,

"You.... where on earth are you from?"

But Jesus still did not answer. Pilate had water brought in a basin and washed his hands in it, then said to the Jews,

"I declare myself innocent of this man's blood."

Then the Jews again waved their fists and said,

"Let that blood be upon us and upon our children!"

Thus the Jews willingly chose to walk the long and endless road of the fig tree.

At last Jesus was transferred from the religious leaders to the Roman governor's court, and then with Pilate's sentence was suddenly handed over to the soldiers. The soldiers took Jesus into the courtyard called the Praetorium, summoned their companions, and began mocking Jesus. They dressed Jesus in a purple outer robe and wove a crown from thorns and placed it on his head, then struck his head and spat on him, while also bowing and saying,

"Hail, King of the Jews."

Jesus was already nothing more than a Messiah, nothing at all. He was now only a prisoner being executed by law. The soldiers, tired of making a fool of Jesus, soon put a cross on him and began leading him out. Barabbas was released as the Passover amnesty, but the remaining two party members also carried their own crosses and followed behind Jesus.

John watched that far and then slipped away quietly. Now everything was over. Jesus had performed no miracle, and nothing had happened. Perhaps, as Annas had said, he was only a somewhat superior magician after all. John began trudging in the opposite direction from Jesus, who was walking toward the execution ground carrying his cross. The three years he had spent following Jesus with a hope that maybe something would happen were dissolving like foam. A sense of emptiness was sinking over his entire body.

(Where.... where am I to go now?)

He still did not feel like going back to his hometown. He did not want to go to that frustrating Sea of Galilee and fish by casting nets.

When a man named Simon, who had come to Jerusalem with his family from the region of Cyrene to meet Jesus, who was rumored to be the Messiah, took up Jesus' struggling cross instead, the disciple John was walking the opposite road and making new plans for his own future.

(Now the way to confront Vespasian.... is only the way of entering the Zealots....)

As John was thinking that, another lightning-like thought suddenly flashed through his mind.

(No. Jesus may yet work a reversal miracle at that place of crucifixion! Jesus, who fed five thousand in the wilderness of Bethsaida, Jesus, who brought back Lazarus who had been dead four days, if he should declare himself the Messiah at the place of execution and proclaim the establishment of the kingdom....)

John was already running fiercely along the road outside the city gate that Jesus had taken. His cooled heart began pounding again. Now there was no Lazarus and no Judas. Peter had vanished, and all the disciples had fled. If Jesus turned dramatically at the place of execution, the new Messianic kingdom would be in John's hands.

When John reached Golgotha, the place of execution, Jesus and the other two Zealots were all already hanging on crosses. Jesus' body was already hanging limp, and the two Zealots were straining every ounce of strength to keep their legs extended and prevent themselves from sagging. If the body sagged, breathing would be blocked and they would suffocate.

The Zealot hanging on Jesus' left side drew in a breath and then shouted toward Jesus in a loud voice.

"We thought you were the Messiah.... are you not the Messiah? If you are the Messiah, shouldn't you save us?"

His voice came mixed with tears. His tears were a lament that held not only himself but also the grievance of all the poor people of his nation. It was a cry of despair confirming once again that the Messiah had not come. Then the Zealot hanging on the right side said in a weak voice,

"Hey.... perhaps our hearts were too hasty.... But though we are in this state because of what we did, what fault does this man have? Jesus.... you always taught the kingdom of heaven.... when you reach that kingdom.... please remember us."

Then Jesus, who had been hanging limply, again put strength into his legs and lifted his head.

"Today.... you will be.... in Paradise with me...."

As he said that and looked down, Jesus discovered John standing there. John was still looking up at the cross with a thread of hope. But Jesus again closed his eyes and went limp, then turned his body once more. And he looked down at the women standing beneath the cross. There were Jesus' mother Mary, Mary Magdalene, Mary the wife of Clopas, and John's mother Salome. Jesus' voice came out faintly.

"Mother.... behold, he.... will become your son."

And then Jesus again turned his weak gaze toward John.

"John.... please serve as your mother."

After that Jesus muttered in a barely audible voice.

"It is.... it is finished...."

That was the end. John, who had run all the way to Golgotha with the last thin hope in his heart, ended up confirming Jesus' end and being left only with his mother to care for.

Because the day Jesus and the two Zealots were executed was the day before the Sabbath, the Jews asked the governor to have the prisoners' legs broken so that the bodies could be dealt with before the Sabbath. If the legs were broken, they could no longer brace their bodies, so they would quickly die of suffocation and the execution procedure could be completed sooner. With Governor Pilate's permission, the soldiers broke the legs of the Zealots with clubs. But Jesus had already sagged and died.

"This one doesn't seem to need to be broken."

"Still, we'd better check just in case."

When one soldier pierced Jesus' side with a spear, his mother Mary screamed and covered her face with both hands. From the side of Jesus that had been pierced with the spear, blood and water were pouring out together.

Shortly afterward, two members of the Sanhedrin appeared on the hill of Golgotha. They were Nicodemus, who had been sent by the council to investigate Jesus, and Joseph of Arimathea, who had opposed Jesus' arrest. Joseph of Arimathea was the one who had begun to take an interest in Jesus after hearing Nicodemus's words. He had been criticized several times in the council for speaking in favor of Jesus, but in the end he had not been able to do anything to stop the council's decision. Nicodemus said to John, making a familiar gesture to him,

"Councillor Joseph has come out for Jesus' burial. He petitioned Governor Pilate to take charge of Jesus' body and obtained permission. There happens to be a new tomb prepared nearby for the Joseph family, so it would be best to move the body there."

In the end, the Galileans who had been arguing over who would have a place when the new government came all fled, and Jesus was buried again by the hands of the people of Jerusalem. John could not even raise his head because he was so ashamed of his own Galilean identity as he watched Jesus being buried by their hands.

In any case, with that, revolution, ambition, and revenge all became foam and vanished. The women who had followed Jesus returned with Jesus' mother Mary, who was crying out in grief, to Mark's house, and John also followed behind them with his shoulders drooping. Jesus' last charge from the cross to care for his mother kept weighing on him again and again.

Peter, who had disappeared from Annas's house, had also returned to Mark's house. He too appeared to have wandered everywhere and come back covered in dust all over his body. With no strength left, he gave John one piece of outside news as he came in.

"...Judas, they say, killed himself."

"Judas...?"

"It seems Judas was.... not denouncing the Teacher for money after all.... Someone says Judas came into the temple weeping, threw the money he had received from Caiaphas into the sanctuary, and ran out.... Judas was found outside the city hanging dead."

Even though such a powerful rival, whom he had guarded against so intensely, had died, John only felt stifled. Now John had no reason left to compete with anyone. Now John and Judas had become the same sort of people, both having lost their goal. Rather, sorrow for Judas's death was coming over John. His considerate figure, who had worried about the future of the nation, kept appearing before his eyes and John kept sighing. But John and Peter were not in any position to be concerned with anyone else's thoughts. Now that Jesus had been executed for treason, they did not know when another order might be issued for their arrest. They hid in Mark's house and spent an anxious Sabbath.

At dawn on the day after the Sabbath, Mary Magdalene, Mary the wife of Clopas, and Salome, John's mother, went out carrying spices to anoint Jesus' body and went to Jesus' tomb. But soon afterward Mary Magdalene came back, breathless. As soon as she returned, she was shouting loudly.

"He has risen! Jesus has risen again!"

John, Peter, and the women rushed out from one room and another. Because Mary Magdalene was so shocked, it was difficult to understand what she was saying, but John quickly organized her words. When they had approached Jesus' tomb, the large stone that had sealed the tomb had already been rolled aside and the tomb stood open. Because Jesus had said long ago that if he died he would rise again in three days, the soldiers had been guarding Jesus' tomb at the request of the Sanhedrin, but they too were nowhere to be seen.

John and Peter could no longer keep listening to Mary Magdalene's explanation. As if burned by fire, they sprang up and began running straight toward Jesus' tomb. The young John was faster than Peter. Moreover, since he knew the tomb of Joseph of Arimathea, where Jesus had been buried, he could run in the lead.

At last, when John reached the tomb and was just about to dive into it, the moment his eyes caught the white linen cloth, he suddenly felt his whole body stiffen.

If Jesus had risen, then John was finished. If he had risen, that would mean Jesus had returned again as the omnipotent one, and then all of John's covert activities, which he had carried out while evading Jesus in order to use him, would be revealed in full before the risen Jesus. John was in despair. He would now stand naked before him.

Peter, who came out of the tomb, was speaking in an excited voice about the Teacher's body having disappeared, while John stood there trembling all over. Just as Peter had fallen before Jesus on the shore of Galilee, John too was falling down a sheer cliff.

John finally managed to finish his long confession with all the strength he had left, then closed his eyes. His face was turning black, and his breathing was very thin. At last Papias put down the reed pen and went over to Prochorus, who was supporting John's back. He was moving to relieve the man who had been supporting John's body on his own back for such a long time. When Prochorus shifted away, John's back touched Papias's back. His body temperature had already fallen considerably. At that moment John opened his mouth again with his eyes still closed.

"His rising again was despair to me.... All my ambition to use him for my own purposes was exposed.... The reason the people of Jerusalem became enraged at him was because of me, who spread the rumor that he would not let the people of Jerusalem into the new government.... It was not Judas who sold him, but me. I was the one who drove him into death for my own purposes.... If he had risen again, I could only stand before him as an unforgivable sinner, as a cursed apostate. With his rising again.... I realized that I who wanted to be first had finally become last...."

Again a deathly silence settled among the people. John's confession, after such a brutal life, was chilling the hearts of everyone as it sank into them.

"I became last.... The risen Lord gathered the scattered disciples again, and they came together in Mark's upper room and devoted themselves to prayer. On the forty-ninth day after the Lord's resurrection, on the Feast of Weeks, the event of the coming of the Holy Spirit finally occurred, and the disciples scattered throughout the whole world and preached the gospel of the kingdom of heaven until they became martyrs.... My brother James became a martyr in Jerusalem.... Matthias in Ethiopia, Peter and Paul in Rome, Andrew in Achaia, Simon and Thaddaeus, who had been Zealot spies, in Persia, Nathanael and Thomas in India, Philip in Hierapolis, and Matthew again in Ethiopia, and his brother James in Egypt all received the glory of martyrdom for preaching the gospel of Christ.... but I became the last...."

Tears gathered in Callimachus's eyes. The life of one man who had lived his whole life amid jealousy and betrayal was making him weep. John opened his eyes and grasped the hand Callimachus held out to him.

"Callimachus.... Jesus said in that final sermon, Agapate allelus.... Love one another...."

He turned his head again toward Andronicus and extended another hand. Andronicus hurried over and took his hand. Holding the hands of the two young men in both of his own hands, he said,

"The kingdoms of this world contend and divide.... in the kingdom of God, one forgives and reconciles with another...."

Callimachus, looking at Andronicus with tear-wet eyes, said,

"Andronicus.... if you can forgive a worthless man like me, please receive your wife back again."
