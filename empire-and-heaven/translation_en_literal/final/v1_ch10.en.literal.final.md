# Empire and Heaven (I)

## Chapter 10. The Mural in the Quarry

In the quarry on the island of Patmos, where the hot sun beat down, the shouts of the guards, the cries of pain, and the blows of hammers breaking stone were breaking together with the waves all day long. The young and strong prisoners were assigned to carry the dressed stones and load them onto the ships anchored at the shore, while the somewhat older prisoners were chiefly put to work dressing stone in the quarry itself.

Among those many prisoners, the old man John, his white hair flying, was chipping stone. The movement with which he set the chisel on the stone, lifted the hammer, and brought it down had now grown very familiar, and upon his naked back the burning sun of the Mediterranean poured down without mercy, so that the skin that had once only been pale now shone black beyond bronze.

Even while bringing down the hammer at a steady pace, his lips moved now and then.

"Lord."

Panting, he said,

"Why is it that You still do not grant me death?"

Amid that great whirlwind of persecutions John had not died; neither had he died beneath the stones thrown by the people of Ephesus; he had lived under the blade of Vespasianus, and had been delivered again even from the oil cauldron of Domitian. And now John had been dragged even to this harsh quarry on the island of Patmos. The hard labor of the quarry was more severe than any other punishment. Even young prisoners, before many months had passed, grew exhausted and died there. Yet in that quarry John was miraculously dragging on his stubborn life.

"Lord."

Again he called into the empty air.

"The life You entrusted to me is too heavy. Even after taking everything from me, why do You not take my life?"

Amid the continuing blows of the hammer, John heard again another voice. It was the very voice he had heard once on the shore at Tiberias.

"Till I come... till I come, tarry thou."

"Have You not... already come? Are You not standing before me like this? Have I not already confessed, as Peter confessed, that I love You?"

Muttering something again and again, he went on hammering. He was so absorbed in striking stone that he did not even hear the bell that marked the noon meal.

"Apostle..."

Only when he heard someone calling him did John stop hammering and raise his head. A curly-haired young man was standing there.

"It is I. Prochorus."

Prochorus too had come with John even to this island of Patmos. He had pleaded with Maximinus, commander of the detachment posted to guard the quarry, and had volunteered for the hard labor of the quarry. His reason was that when a task too heavy for the old apostle John's strength was assigned, he could help with the work.

Only then did John at last set down the chisel and hammer, stand up, and straighten his back. There came from his waist a cracking sound. The cut face of the rock line they had been carving spread out before his eyes like a white screen.

Still casting his gaze upon that white blankness, he opened his mouth.

"Prochorus, why do you not listen to me and leave this island, instead of wasting your time here?"

"It is not wasted. You, apostle, are now the last guardian who keeps Jesus Christ, and I am the watchman who keeps the apostle."

"Prochorus, there is too much work in the world for young men to do. You are precious workers who must prepare the way for the coming of Jesus Christ."

"The brothers in Ephesus entrusted this task to me."

He could not bring himself to repeat openly the request of Papias and Polycarp. They had told Prochorus to follow every word and action of John without fail and write it down. It was a fierce struggle between John, who wished to keep silence, and the younger disciples, who wished to learn even one thing more from him.

"Apostle, you must go and receive your food."

John rubbed the fingers of both hands together to shake off the stone dust, then unfastened the bowl and spoon hanging at his waist and took them in hand. In the palms of the old apostle, harder even than stone dust, thick calluses had formed.

From childhood John had in truth grown up without knowing much hardship. Though he had fished in Capernaum, his father Zebedee had been an owner of several boats, and so all the heavy tasks were done by hired fishermen like Peter, while John and James did little more than assist them. Even after Jesus' execution, while many Christiani suffered in the midst of persecution, John had still lived comparatively better than they, because he bore the responsibility of caring for Mary, the mother of Jesus, and so always fled to safe places, while the saints sent offerings first to him so that he should lack nothing. The only labor he had really done was in his old age, when he had tried a little vegetable farming in the grove of Ortygia in order to help the orphans of Ephesus.

For such a John, the hard labor of this quarry was an immense change of circumstance. Yet by now John's obedience had become deeply habitual. He had come to think that until God called him, there was nothing to do but follow His direction in whatever came. Whenever meal time came, he always gave thanks to God.

(Thank You.... If I had stood before the Lord without knowing this place, how ashamed I would have been?)

The meal, as always, was porridge boiled with wheat and barley together with vegetables. John and Prochorus each received a bowl, sat down on the gravel, and offered a prayer of thanksgiving.

It was then. Prochorus tugged at the apostle's sleeve. Maximinus, commander of the quarry guard detachment, was coming lumbering toward them with his great body swaying.

To the prisoners, the quarry guard was an object of terror. Just as the quarry was the worst place of exile for prisoners, so for soldiers the most dreaded assignment was duty at the quarry. Therefore the soldiers sent to this quarry guard detachment, whether officers or common soldiers, were men who had caused trouble or had been judged incompetent.

For that reason the guards were all the more violent and savage toward the prisoners. Included in such conduct was the pent-up rage they felt over having themselves been thrown away like refuse. And in the hand of Maximinus, the commander now approaching the place where John and Prochorus sat, there was as always a leather whip. Among the guards he was the foremost master of the lash.

John, as though unconcerned with Maximinus, kept lifting spoonfuls of potato porridge to his mouth. At length the large leather boots of the detachment commander came right up before John's eyes, and from above his head there came the man's voice.

"I hear you are a leader among the Christiani...."

Only then did John lift his head and look up into the big face of Maximinus. Strangely enough, today his harsh face had grown much softer. John swallowed the lump of potato in his mouth and answered.

"I could not be called a leader... but I am among the oldest."

At that Maximinus glanced around for a moment, then squatted down before John and said,

"I hear you are a Jew... have you perhaps ever seen Jesus of Nazareth?"

"...?"

Prochorus, who was listening beside them, cut in.

"The Apostle John is a disciple who served Jesus of Nazareth directly."

Maximinus knit his brows for a moment, sank into thought, and then, as though making up his mind, said,

"My wife too is a Jew like you... and I have heard that this man Jesus in whom you believe healed hard illnesses such as palsy and leprosy by prayer. Is that true?"

His tone had grown more respectful than at first. Looking steadily into his face, John replied,

"It is true. Wherever He went, He comforted the poor, healed the sick, and made those possessed by demons whole."

"And... I have heard that His disciples too displayed the same miracles."

"It was not the disciples who did it, but it was done in the name of Jesus Christ."

But Maximinus suddenly seized John's rough hand in his thick hands and pleaded in an earnest voice:

"Sir, I beg you. My only son is dying of a fever. Please come and help him."

Maximinus was pulling at John's hand with his great hands, and John remained seated there, hesitating a moment. In truth, although he had prayed for many people up to now, he had never experienced the kind of astonishing miracles that Peter or Paul had performed.

Jesus of Nazareth had always made sure to take Peter and James, and also John himself, into important moments. John had already been astonished at the wedding feast in Cana when he saw Jesus turn water into wine, but he had been even more astonished when He raised up the dead daughter of Jairus, the ruler of the synagogue in Capernaum. Even then Jesus had taken only Peter, James, and John with Him into the house and had lifted that twelve-year-old girl by the hand.

Such miracles of Jesus were in fact too many to record one by one. Jesus of Nazareth was a man who could do everything except save Himself from the cross.

Yet the miracles of Jesus did not end with Jesus alone. Fifty days after His resurrection, on Pentecost, a few days after the strange event of the descent of the Holy Spirit in Mark's upper room, John experienced again that astonishing miracle.

On that day too John was going up to the temple in Jerusalem with Peter for the afternoon prayer. Then a lame beggar sitting by the temple gate caught hold of Peter's garment. And Peter, with boldness, took the lame man's hand and cried out,

"Silver and gold have I none; but such as I have give I thee: in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth rise up and walk!"

And he lifted him up. Astonishingly, the lame man not only stood and walked, but leaped and praised God. In an instant this matter became known throughout all Jerusalem, and once again the religious leaders had to devise measures in order to persecute the believers in Jesus.

But the one more astonished than anyone was John, who had gone with Peter. John had always been the disciple who served Jesus at His side, and he himself had thought of himself as the chief disciple. More than that, because Peter had been a hired man in his father's household, John had always regarded him as a companion standing one step beneath himself.

Yet when he saw this immense miracle, John was overwhelmed. And later, when not only Peter but even Paul, who had never directly seen Jesus, was going about on missionary journeys performing miracle after miracle, John felt a bitter humiliation and was concerned only with keeping himself hidden.

That humiliation regarding miracles had made John almost afraid of miracle itself. He always put Peter before him and hid himself behind. Thus John's path was always one of stepping back, a path in the shadow. And it was also a quiet sign of the hidden dissatisfaction John felt toward Jesus, who seemed to give His gifts unfairly.

Thus John's faith, more than a faith of miracles, had begun to ripen into a faith of obedience, a silent faith that endured through love and patience. And now this huge soldier was asking from such a John a prayer for miracle.

"You said it was a fever?"

"That is right. Sir, please save my son. Because I took a Jewish wife, I have lived suffering every humiliation. I gave up advancement, and I was driven out to this barren island. The only reason I lived was by placing my hope in my child. And now that son is dying."

Fever was a terrible disease for which even physicians had no cure. Moreover, because it was contagious, the more pressing question was not how to save the sick man but how to isolate him from the rest.

John thought of what had happened in Capernaum when Peter's mother-in-law was struck by fever. Then too Peter's mother-in-law had given up on treatment and was lying isolated, only waiting for the day of her death. Yet Jesus of Nazareth had gone into that house, though no one wished to enter it.

And then with a gentle hand He had raised her up.

(Lord... have mercy on this pitiful soldier.)

At last John rose from his place. He had been moved by the desperate pleading of Maximinus. Since he had already been driven to this island for no other reason than marrying a Jew, if it should become known above that he had taken a Christianus into his house, there was no knowing what fresh evil might fall upon him.

After entrusting the afternoon work to his adjutant, Maximinus led John and Prochorus to the commander's quarters on the opposite side of the quarry.

Inside the quarters, everything was in disorder. The weary life of a soldier who had given up advancement, and of his family, had sunk into every corner of the house. In the dark room lay a boy whose face had gone black and thin, his eyes closed, and beside the bed sat a woman with disordered hair, staring blankly in shock. Maximinus introduced his wife to John. Because the master of the house was so huge, the narrow room seemed all the poorer.

"Sir, this is my wife Milcah. Wife, this man is the very direct disciple of Jesus of Nazareth. He will heal our son."

The woman called Milcah looked at John with shining eyes. First John spoke words of comfort to her.

"Do not be so downcast, madam."

John came up to the boy and laid his hand on his forehead. The forehead was burning like a fireball. Thinking of Jesus as He had been while in this world, he opened his mouth.

"While You were in this world, You promised that whatever we should ask in Your name, You would do it. Now this old sinner remembers Your promise; have pity on this child...."

John remembered that promise Jesus had spoken at the Last Supper.

"You also said, 'I will not leave you comfortless: I will come to you.' If You are now here, I believe that when my heart aches, Yours too will ache with it."

Tears had gathered in John's eyes.

"Please heal this child. In the name of our Lord Jesus Christ I ask it."

Heavy silence filled the room. John's trembling hand was still on the child's forehead.

(?)

John realized that an indescribable power was coming upon the hand he had laid upon the child's brow. Beads of sweat were beginning to rise on John's forehead. It was then. Directly from under John's hand there came the boy's voice.

"Mother!"

Maximinus was startled, and the boy's mother Milcah was startled, and Prochorus was startled. But the one most astonished of all was John himself. The child's forehead beneath his hand was growing cool.

Maximinus's wife rushed forward and embraced the child, while John and Prochorus knelt and gave thanks. Hot tears were running down John's face.

That very day Maximinus, his wife, and his son, the three of them, received baptism from John and became Christiani. Prochorus expressed concern over the soldier's future as a soldier, but Maximinus himself was rather carefree, saying that his military career had long since been ruined anyway. From now on, he said, he would instead place his hope in the heavenly kingdom awaited by the Christiani.

But among them all, John's emotion could not be described. Up until now he had in truth lived with the feeling that he had been cast off by Jesus, his beloved Teacher. To Peter, who anxiously asked what would become of John, Jesus had answered:

"If I will that he tarry till I come, what is that to thee?"

When so many companions had embraced the glory of martyrdom, the opportunity had not been given to him. And now, though he had grown old and gone all the way to Rome, still he had not been allowed to die. John had given up hope of being forgiven by Jesus. When he had been with Him, he had not been true to Him. Yet the more he tried to forget, the more painfully the memories of Jesus came alive within him.

And now, after so long a time, upon this desolate island of Patmos, he had met Jesus again. Unable to contain his excitement, he returned to the quarry and began again to hammer. Without ceasing he muttered:

(The Teacher remembered me. He came to me....)

Even after all the other prisoners had finished work and returned to their barracks, John remained kneeling on the floor of the quarry, offering prayer. Thus John's prayer continued. Without even taking food, work and prayer, prayer and work, went on for two days, and at last the third day came.

That day happened to be a day of rest. For prisoners and guards alike it was a day to rest their weary bodies, and for the Christiani it was the Lord's Day.

John went to the quarters of Commander Maximinus and held morning worship with the five of them, including that family of three, and then went straight once more to the quarry. And once again the prayer began. About the time when the stinging sun had moved slowly past overhead and the red light of sunset was falling again, John, kneeling toward the sea, suddenly heard behind him the great sound of a trumpet sweeping down upon him.

Startled, John turned and said,

"Who... who are You?"

Then red flame began to blaze upon the white rock wall cut by the quarrymen. And mingled with the trumpet sound there rang out a great voice.

"I am Alpha and Omega...."

"Ah!"

"...which is, and which was, and which is to come, the Almighty."

John's whole body was trembling like an aspen tree. There, in the burning flame upon the white cliff, there appeared like a mural seven golden lampstands. And among the lampstands there appeared the form of a man. He was clothed in a garment down to the foot, and girded about the breast with a golden band; His hair was white like wool, and His eyes burned like fire.

John fell down upon the spot and could no longer lift his head. Again there came to his ears a voice like the sound of clear waters.

"Fear not. I am the first and the last: I am he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for evermore, and have the keys of death and of Hades. Write therefore the things which thou hast seen, and the things which are, and the things which shall be hereafter...."

John lifted his head again and looked around on every side. There still stood behind him his disciple Prochorus, with both hands joined.

"Prochorus... go quickly and prepare papyrus and ink and reed pens. The revelation of the Most High must be written down!"

At once Prochorus ran around the quarry toward the village. While he had gone to fetch writing materials, the secrets of the seven churches began to appear upon the white cliff. Like a mural, the vision appearing on the rock wall was showing the problems that were about to come upon the churches.

In the first church appeared the struggle with heresy; the second church was stained with terrible suffering; in the third church could be seen fallen prophets turning back toward the world. At last, in the fourth church, idolatry and fornication began to appear; the fifth church had become filthy and was rotting away. By the sixth, the church seemed to regain its proper form, but as it entered the seventh church it again began to be defiled. And before that seventh church there had already arrived one clothed in white. Before the seventh church, rich on the side of the world but poor on the side of the heavenly kingdom, that white-clad one had already laid hold upon the handle of the door.

When Prochorus returned with the writing materials, John at once began to dictate.

"I, John, write this letter to the seven churches that are in Asia. Grace be unto you, and peace, from Him which is, and which was, and which is to come; and from the seven Spirits which are before His throne; and from Jesus Christ, who is the faithful witness, and the first begotten from the dead, and the ruler of the kings of the earth. Amen."

Both John's voice as he dictated while looking toward the wall of the quarry, and Prochorus's hand as he wrote it down, were trembling. Like a prophet of old, John raised both hands and said,

"He cometh with clouds... and every eye shall see Him... and they also which pierced Him... and all kindreds of the earth shall wail because of Him.... Even so... Amen."

Then it seemed that the mural of the quarry wall was fading away, and again the throne of heaven appeared. Upon the throne sat radiant glory, and around it were seated twenty-four elders clothed in white garments. While the twenty-four elders and the angels praised Him who sat upon the throne, at last the One seated there held up a book sealed seven times, and Jesus of Nazareth came forward before Him, took the book, and broke the seals.

The seven seals meant seven stages of great tribulation prepared in order to set right the world that had gone astray.

When the first seal was broken, what appeared on the wall was a rider upon a white horse. The tribulation that was to come upon the world, irretrievably defiled, was beginning from truth itself. The rider on the white horse shot countless arrows of truth, and the world began to suffer in pain.

When the second seal was broken, there appeared a rider on a red horse. He removed peace from the earth and left only hatred and slaughter.

Third there appeared a rider on a black horse. This rider diminished the produce of the earth and raised the price of grain. All men upon the earth became hired laborers and were falling into slavery in order to live.

Fourth there appeared a rider on a pale horse. He was pouring out upon the earth dreadful weapons, famine, and death.

When Jesus of Nazareth broke the fifth seal, the Christiani remaining on the earth began to suffer tribulation. And after they had at last disappeared from the earth, things truly dreadful began to appear. They began in the sixth stage. A great earthquake came upon the earth, and the sun became black as sackcloth, while the moon turned to blood, and the stars of heaven were falling down.

Before Jesus of Nazareth had yet broken the seventh seal, John saw once more a marvelous thing. It was the very scene of the salvation of the Jews.

The Jews, who had gone on suffering ever since the kingdom split in two after Solomon. They had been trampled by Assyria, taken by Babylon, passed over to the Medes and Persians, and then humiliated by Alexander. After Alexander's death, his successors had thoroughly crushed the pride of the Jews, and when Judea seemed to revive under the resistance of the house of Maccabee, she was again violated by the mighty power of the Roman Empire.

That people, God's firstborn, chosen by God and living as the people of promise from unimaginably ancient times, the people who even in those hard sufferings trusted the promise of God and kept the commandments with their lives. John still had not been able to resolve the question of that tragedy which befell Jerusalem.

Moreover, Jesus of Nazareth had poured out unspeakable rebukes upon the religious leaders of Jerusalem. The leaders of Jerusalem, hearing His rebukes, had been bewildered and at a loss. So they came to think that they had to remove this stranger who had suddenly appeared. And for that flurried response, the punishment of God had been unspeakably severe. On this point John had been unable to find an answer, and had prayed with tears.

Yet today this revelation appearing on the cliff was showing the salvation of the Jews. At last John understood greatly, and his heart was being refreshed as though a great opening had been made within it.

Jehovah, God of justice and also God of love. He simply would not leave the world over to the powers of rebellion. Yet if He was to forgive the sins of the world, compensation was required through His own suffering. That was the sending of His own Son Jesus. Thus Jesus became the firstborn of all the citizens of the heavenly kingdom.

But the power of Satan lodged an accusation against God. It protested that it was unjust to forgive human sin through God's own pain alone. At last God resolved to sacrifice the Jewish people, the firstborn among all nations, as the people who would share in His suffering.

Thus Jesus came as the representative of this suffering firstborn. He rebuked and denounced the Jewish people who were to be His companions in pain. It was pain directed toward Himself. At last John understood why Jesus wept as He looked upon Jerusalem before entering it. The people of Israel had borne the cross together with Jesus in order to carry the sin of the world. John again repeated in his mind the writing of the prophet Isaiah, who had prophesied concerning Jesus:

Surely He hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows:
yet we did esteem Him stricken, smitten of God, and afflicted.
But He was wounded for our transgressions,
He was bruised for our iniquities:
the chastisement of our peace was upon Him;
and with His stripes we are healed.
All we like sheep have gone astray;
we have turned every one to his own way;
and the Lord hath laid on Him the iniquity of us all.
He was oppressed, and He was afflicted,
yet He opened not His mouth:
He is brought as a lamb to the slaughter,
and as a sheep before her shearers is dumb,
so He opened not His mouth.

There was no knowing how much suffering still remained for the Jews scattered in every place. Nor could anyone know how much longer they must live in affliction. The Jews had all now been sold as slaves in places such as Ephesus and Rome and Alexandria. And the heavier the sin of the world became, the greater also would become the suffering of the Jews. In the end, the second coming of Jesus had to come through the awakening to truth itself. When men come to know the truth that another person's suffering is in fact because of me, then Jesus of Nazareth comes again on the clouds.

At last Jesus of Nazareth was breaking the seventh seal. Upon the wall of the quarry there appeared seven angels. And each of the seven angels held a trumpet.

When the first angel sounded, one-third of the earth and of the trees was burned. When the second angel sounded, one-third of the sea was polluted, and the creatures of the sea died in masses. Again, when the third angel sounded, one-third of the rivers and even of the springs was polluted, and many died from drinking the foul water. And with the fourth angel's sounding, the sun, moon, and stars all at once began to grow dark. Next the fifth angel sounded. All manner of dreadful ideas and doctrines on the earth began to threaten men, harry them, and choke them. Amid loud groaning and crying from the people, the sixth trumpet sounded. The four great powers on the earth at last began to move their armies, and from the East an army of two hundred million was advancing toward the Euphrates. Strange weapons on land and sea and in the air poured out fire, and one-third of mankind dwelling upon the earth was dying in that war. And at last the seventh angel came forward holding the trumpet.

Already on the earth the power of the world was making mankind altogether its slaves. The ruler of the earth was causing all men to receive a number on their foreheads, and without that number no one could buy or sell anything. Wherever they went, men had to go about bearing a number. Without the number, nothing could be done. Every action of men was recorded together with that number, and at last the name of the person who received the number was being erased from the register of the heavenly kingdom. And the numbered people were making speaking machines carved from stone and overlaid with gold and silver, and were living according to the command of those things.

At last the seventh angel sounded the trumpet he held. The temple of God in heaven was opened, and there was seen the ark of Jehovah's covenant, which had vanished together with the destruction of the temple of Jerusalem.

And upon the wall of the quarry there appeared another seven angels. Each of them held to his breast a great bowl. When the first angel poured out his bowl on the earth, malignant tumors arose upon those who had received the number of the idol; when the second angel poured out his bowl, the whole sea was polluted and every sea creature died; and with the pouring out of the third bowl, all the rivers, even to their springs, were polluted, and drinking water disappeared.

When the fourth angel poured out his bowl, strange changes came in the climate, and all living things on the earth were scorched to death by the sun. When the fifth angel poured out his bowl, men bit their tongues in pain because of those tumors. When the sixth bowl was poured out, mankind's final war began to be prepared in a place called Armageddon.

And then at last the seventh bowl was poured upon the earth. Amid great lightning and thunder there came an earthquake, and the earth split open, and the crust of the world was changed.

At length the cities of the world, giving off the smell of rot, were sinking into the sea. All the gold, silver, jewels, silks, vessels, perfumes, and strong drink that had gathered into the cities were all sinking down together, along with the screams of the merchants who had bought and sold them.

When all the foul smoke and stench had vanished, again upon the wall of the quarry the armies of heaven came marching forth. And at their head stood Jesus of Nazareth upon a white horse. Bloodstains still remained upon the fine linen He wore, yet upon the hem of that garment there was clearly inscribed the word Truth. The army following Him also was made up entirely of warriors clothed in white fine linen like snow, riding white horses.

"O Lord!"

John could bear it no longer, and staggered to his feet. The first heaven and the first earth were gone, and the new heaven and the new earth were appearing. At last one age had passed away, and a new age was beginning. Jesus upon the white horse was washing the hands of all who came forth victorious out of suffering.

From heaven descended the new Jerusalem. It was not that wretched Jerusalem that had been surrounded by Titus, starved for three years, and finally driven to roast and eat her own child; it was a city of peace and love. It was not Jerusalem where the water of rotting corpses ran, but the city of glory ruled by God and by His Son Jesus of Nazareth.

The triumphal procession of Jesus of Nazareth was drawing nearer and nearer. No one could stop His way.

"O Lord!"

John flung himself forward.

"You know how I waited for You, do You not? You know how I waited for You and wept in the dark places of this world, do You not?"

John was dancing for joy. He was dancing and dancing.

"I knew it would come to this, I knew it would come to this."

Still dancing, he was singing the song of Moses:

Lord God Almighty,
great and marvelous are Thy works;
King of nations,
just and true are Thy ways.
Lord, who shall not fear Thy name,
and glorify Thee?
For Thou only art holy:
for Thy righteous acts have been made manifest;
and all nations shall come
and worship before Thee.

When John, having danced until he was short of breath, knelt again upon the ground, there came the voice like a trumpet:

"I am Alpha and Omega, the first and the last."

"Lord, I give thanks and praise."

John joined his two hands together. It was a truly astonishing experience. Sweat was running on the brow of Prochorus, who sat beside him taking down the words. While still a radiant light lingered on the wall of the quarry, there came another voice:

"Surely I come quickly."

John rose once more to his feet, lifted both hands, and cried out in a loud voice:

"Maranatha!"

It was Aramaic, and it meant, "Lord, come."
