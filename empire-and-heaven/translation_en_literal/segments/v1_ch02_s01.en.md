All the passengers of the Vesta had poured out onto the deck and were gazing, entranced, at Ephesus, which shone beautifully in the evening sunlight. Someone muttered as though groaning, "Oh, beautiful jewel of Artemis...."

In the middle of the harbor of Ephesus, gilded with gold, stood the beautiful form of the harbor monument composed of soft Ionic columns, and behind it the broad Harbor Avenue stretched straight ahead, cutting across the heart of the city of Ephesus toward the great theater spread out in the shape of a fan. Mount Pion, rising softly like a woman's breast as it embraced that great theater, which could hold twenty-four thousand people in its sixty-six tiers of stepped seating, was also filled with the evening sun.

"Ah! The goddess Artemis is coming out!"

When someone cried out so, everyone stretched out their necks and looked toward the harbor.

"Where, where?"

"Look over there. She is coming out from the road on the right side of the great theater!"

What they first noticed was the crowd pouring out from the direction of the great theater. Countless people began to fill the road, and the crowd in turn was covered with flowers and leaves.

"We are fortunate. We have arrived at the spring festival of Artemis!"

Filling that broad Harbor Avenue, the crowd was gradually moving toward the harbor. Thus the moment when the Vesta dropped anchor at the quay and the moment when the festival crowd passed through the harbor monument were almost the same. Through the monument's three exits, the straight central gate and the arched gates to left and right, people were pouring out like a flood tide.

Because the quay was so noisy, Andronicus and Hesthenes had not yet thought of disembarking and were standing at the gunwale. And because it was actually better to remain on the ship if one wished to watch the festival, they were looking down at the quay from aboard.

At last the image of the goddess Artemis, surrounded by flowers and leaves, began to pass through the central gate. At the very front stood the high priest dressed in white, and behind him male priests walked out guarding the divine image. All of them were guards of the goddess who had cut off their own testicles with their own hands and buried them beneath the altar of Artemis. Behind them followed female priests, also dressed in white. The number of the priestesses, who had to spend their whole lives in virginity, was twenty-four, the same as the number of Artemis's breasts.

Originally, this spring festival procession began at the temple of Artemis in the marshland behind Mount Pion. The image of Artemis, coming out from the temple and leading the procession of the ecstatic crowd, entered the city of Ephesus from the east through the Magnesian Gate, passed the eastern gymnasium and the Tetragonos market, went by the front of the odeon, and came to stand before the city hall of Ephesus. Inside that city hall another image of Artemis stood looking outward, and when the crowd's frenzy reached its peak between the two divine images, the governor of Ephesus was to come out from his residence and pay homage to the goddess. After receiving the governor's greeting, the goddess would begin moving again, pass along Curetes Street, go by the front of the library, and reach the square of Ephesus. Then, in front of the square, she would turn left and enter Harbor Avenue. When the goddess passed down Harbor Avenue and reached the quay, the sacrifice of bulls and the festival would finally begin; when the festival ended, the procession would enter again toward the great theater, turn left before the theater, pass the great stadium, and go through the Koressos Gate. When the image of the goddess passed through the Koressos Gate, went around the western slope of Mount Pion, turned north again, and reached the temple, this noisy spring festival day would come to an end.

"Look. At last the blood sacrifice is beginning!"

In the direction Hesthenes pointed, many bulls could be seen. The bulls were fastened among logs woven crosswise and lengthwise so that they could not move at all. Andronicus knew that all those bulls were being offered to Artemis, and even that their number was twenty-four.

Soon the desperate cries of the bulls began to ring out, and the crowd began to burst into ecstatic shouts. The male priests had just begun to cut off the bulls' testicles with sharp knives. Amid blood-colored cries, the severed testicles of the bulls were washed in perfumed oil, threaded onto cords, and began to be hung around the neck of Artemis.

At last, when the testicles of all twenty-four bulls had been hung around the goddess's neck, naked dancers began to dance to music surging as though it would burst apart. Flames blazed on the altar prepared for roasting meat, and the bulls, streaming bright blood from between their legs, were slaughtered one by one and laid upon the altar.

Soon the smell of burning meat rose over the whole quay, and it too drifted out toward the sea.
