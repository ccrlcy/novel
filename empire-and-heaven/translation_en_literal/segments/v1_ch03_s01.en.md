Even after the red sun had been swallowed into the Mediterranean, the steps of the great theater still held a faint light. Along both sides of the straight avenue stretching toward the harbor, the lamps of the shops began to be lit one by one, and from far off in the direction of the harbor the loud sound of fireworks still burst and echoed.

About halfway up the steps of the great theater, where the grains of darkness had begun to settle like dregs, three young men had been standing for some time looking down below. Of them, the somewhat taller young man looked at the youth whose face was clear as a carving and opened his mouth.

"Callimachus... I wish there were some way I could help you."

The youth called Callimachus gazed at the seductive colors of the many-colored lanterns beginning to glow in the direction of the harbor and smiled coldly.

"Do not worry too much, Polycarp. I am truly sorry to show such weakness. Ever since we were children, you have always played the part of comforting me, and I have always been the one receiving comfort...."

"But, Callimachus... we live with hope. I believe that saying, that when we are weak, then we are strongest."

For another while a heavy silence passed over them. Suddenly the young man with curly hair and broad shoulders pointed downward with his finger.

"It looks as though he is coming up this way."

Then the three young men fixed their eyes once more on the avenue below. The finger of the curly-haired youth was indicating the figure of the old man who, with a fish in his hand, was coming straight up toward the theater. Polycarp nodded.

"It seems our observation was correct."

Unlike when he had been wandering aimlessly, the old man's pace was rather swift. In an instant he crossed the square of Ephesus, went over the marble road, and entered the theater. Polycarp spoke again in a low voice.

"Now then... since it has come this far, I think it would be better for us to go down rather than make the old gentleman walk any farther."

They began to descend the steps together. In the dim darkness, the distance between the three young men and the old man grew steadily smaller. The old man passed the three-story stage building made up of dressing rooms and prop rooms and was entering the semicircular forecourt, the open place where the choir and the band would enter. At last, in that semicircular court, the old man came face to face with the young men who had descended the steps.

For a moment they stood there, looking at one another in the middle of the court. At length Polycarp stepped forward and spoke.

"Excuse me... what is that which you are holding in your hand?"

The old man gazed steadily at Polycarp's face, dimly pale in the darkness, and answered in a low voice.

"Ichthys."

Ichthys was the Greek word for fish. Polycarp asked again,

"Do you know the ichthys?"

Then the old man spelled out the letters of ichthys and made of them a short confession.

"Jesus Christ, Theou Huios, Soter."

That is, Jesus Christ is the Son of God and the Savior. It was the password by which Christians recognized one another. The moment his words ended, Polycarp rushed forward and seized the old man's rough and bony hand.

"Welcome. How have you come to this Ephesus? I am called Polycarp, and I live here. This man is my friend named Callimachus, and..."

He pointed to the curly-haired young man standing beside him and continued.

"This man is called Prochorus. All of us are workers of the church in Ephesus."

"It has been so long since I left Ephesus that I have never heard the names of the young men here. Were your fathers all Christians?"

"The grandfather of this Prochorus is Prochorus, one of the first seven deacons of the Jerusalem church. He had been bishop of the church in Nicomedia and passed away not long ago. And..."

With one hand Polycarp took hold of Callimachus's shoulder.

"The father of Callimachus was originally a very zealous Christian."

"That means... he is not so now?"

"Well..."

Before Polycarp could answer, the old man asked Callimachus,

"Your father's honored name...?"

"It is Onesiphorus."

The old man nodded and asked again.

"I see. And where is Master Onesiphorus now?"

"I am told that he died of illness before I was even born."

"Onesiphorus... he was a good man indeed...."

As Callimachus lowered his head and sank into thought, Polycarp quickly changed the subject.

"But... whom have you come to meet in Ephesus, sir?"

"I am a man who has returned to Ephesus after thirty years. Could you lead me to a place where I may meet believing brethren? Who is now responsible for the church in Ephesus?"

"Yes, it is Bishop Onesimus."

"Ah, Onesimus...."

The old man's voice, repeating the name of Onesimus, trembled again with emotion.

"Onesimus... could he perhaps be the same man who came from the church in Colossae?"
