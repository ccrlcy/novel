## Unresolved terminology or consistency risks

- `dominūs / dominus`: I kept the Latin title as `dominus` and glossed it in context as `master`. If later chapters prefer `my lord` or fully English `master`, this should be standardized across the project.
- Roman-name register: I leaned toward familiar English historical forms for major figures (`Octavian`, `Vespasian`, `Domitian`, `Antony`) while leaving some less familiar names in transliterated form (`Hesthenes`, `Drusiana`, `Tychicus`, `Arcadiane`, `Prytaneion`). Future chapters should confirm whether to keep this mixed strategy.
- Biblical references: I preserved `John` and `Jesus` without adding interpretive labels like `John the Baptist` or `Jesus Christ` in order to stay close to the source. If the project later wants stronger Christian-reader signaling, that should be a deliberate series-wide choice.
- Administrative titles: `quaestor`, `aedile`, `praetor`, `consul`, `pontifex maximus`, `delatores`, and `imperium` were kept in historically recognizable forms. A glossary pass may still be useful if the literal line is kept for publication.
- Harsh-content preservation: violent and morally dark material was retained because the brief required preserving material content. If a later editorial pass wants softer phrasing, that should happen in a separate adaptation layer, not in this literal file.
