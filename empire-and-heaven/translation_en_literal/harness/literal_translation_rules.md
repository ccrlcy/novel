# Literal Translation Rules

## Core Rule

Translate the chapter completely. Improve English expression where needed, but do not remove content.

## Required

- Preserve every scene, event, and argument
- Preserve paragraph order unless English grammar makes a tiny local inversion necessary
- Preserve theological claims and biblical weight
- Preserve historical exposition even when dense
- Preserve emotional and rhetorical movement in dialogue
- Keep names and major proper nouns consistent

## Allowed

- Smooth stiff phrasing into natural English
- Break very long Korean sentences into readable English sentences
- Add a small in-line clarifier once when a title or term would otherwise confuse the reader
- Choose standard English biblical phrasing when it preserves meaning more accurately

## Forbidden

- Summarizing historical passages
- Collapsing repeated ideas into one sentence
- Softening conflict by deleting sharp lines
- Turning direct speech into paraphrase
- Removing social or cultural detail because it feels old-fashioned
- Replacing difficult theology with vague spirituality
- Importing plot details from `adaptation_en` that are not supported by the Korean source

## Audience

The target reader is an American Christian adult reader who can handle historical, biblical, and early-church language as long as the English is clean and readable.

## Tone

- Clear
- Serious
- Historically textured
- Biblically literate
- Not slangy
- Not preachy
- Not archaic for its own sake

## Term Handling

- Keep Roman titles such as `dominus`, `quaestor`, and `imperium` when they carry specific historical meaning
- Prefer first-use context over footnote-style explanation
- Keep scriptural and theological terms recognizable to an English-speaking church reader
- Follow `translation_en_literal/harness/term_policy.md` for series-wide consistency
