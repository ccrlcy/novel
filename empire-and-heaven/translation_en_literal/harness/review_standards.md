# Review Standards

## Purpose

The review stage exists to decide whether a literal chapter draft is safe to promote to `final/` without losing source meaning, narrative continuity, or theological weight.

## Review Principle

- Review the Korean source and the English draft together
- Treat the Korean chapter as the only authority for content
- Do not accept omission as a style choice
- Do not accept compression as improvement
- Do not replace difficult theology with easier theology
- Do not silently normalize names, titles, or references without checking series continuity

## Review Questions

1. Is every paragraph represented?
2. Is every scene and speech block present?
3. Has any argument, repetition, or transition been cut?
4. Are the names and titles aligned with the program term policy?
5. Does the chapter preserve the same emotional and theological pressure as the source?
6. Is the ending still pointing into the next chapter in the same way?

## Hard Failures

- Missing paragraphs
- Merged or skipped speech blocks
- Summarized doctrine or historical explanation
- Invented content
- Imported content from `adaptation_en`
- Broken continuity with adjacent chapters

## Soft Risks

- Awkward first-use terminology
- Unclear pronouns in long dialogue
- OCR or source ambiguity that needs a note
- A phrase that is literal but may need a later series consistency pass

## Promotion Rule

A chapter may move to `final/` only when the review note states that no major omission remains open and the chapter is safe for final publication handling.
