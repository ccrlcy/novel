# Literal Translation Term Policy

This policy keeps terminology stable across the full literal translation program.

## 1. Roman Titles And Institutions

- Keep historically specific Latin terms in English text when they carry distinct meaning
- Preferred forms:
  - `dominus`
  - `dominus et deus`
  - `quaestor`
  - `aedile`
  - `praetor`
  - `consul`
  - `imperium`
  - `delatores`
  - `Pax Romana`
- On first use, a short in-line clarifier is allowed if the sentence would otherwise become confusing

## 2. Biblical And Early Church Names

- Use familiar English Christian forms for canonical biblical figures and widely recognized church-historical figures
- Preferred examples:
  - `Jesus`
  - `John`
  - `John the Baptist` only when the source context requires disambiguation
  - `Pontius Pilate`
  - `Herod Antipas`
  - `Mary`
  - `Paul`
  - `Josephus`
  - `Eusebius`

## 3. Novel-Specific Proper Names

- Keep the established Anglicized/transliterated forms already present in the project unless the Korean source clearly requires otherwise
- Preferred examples:
  - `Andronicus`
  - `Hesthenes`
  - `Drusiana`
  - `Tychicus`
  - `Callimachus`
  - `Polycarp`
  - `Prochorus`
  - `Onesimus`
  - `Papias`

## 3.1 Core Series Terms

- Use these forms consistently across the literal program:
  - `Christianus` for singular
  - `Christiani` for plural
  - `Christiana` for singular feminine
  - `Harbor Avenue`
  - `Lord's Day`
  - `Mother of God`
  - `Lugdunum`
  - `Eusebius`
  - `Tyrannus`
  - `Domitianus`
  - `Vespasianus`
  - `Titus`
  - `Nero`
  - `Constantine`
- Keep `Christus`, `Christos`, and `Messiah` distinct when the source makes a theological or historical distinction
- Keep `eucharisteo` as the rite name when the source is referring to the liturgical act rather than a generic thanksgiving
- Use `my lord` for direct honorific address in dialogue, and reserve `master` for literal household or ownership contexts

## 4. Place Names

- Prefer familiar English historical spellings when there is a widely accepted standard
- Keep less familiar classical forms consistently once chosen
- Examples already in use:
  - `Ephesus`
  - `Aegean Sea`
  - `Asia Minor`
  - `Peloponnese`
  - `Arcadiane`
  - `Prytaneion`
  - `Cayster`
  - `Lugdunum`

## 5. Series Rule

- Do not switch between `master`, `my lord`, and `dominus` arbitrarily
- Do not alternate between multiple English spellings of the same major figure without a source-based reason
- If a later chapter requires a better standard, update this file first and then normalize all affected draft chapters
